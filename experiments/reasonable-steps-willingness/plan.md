# Experiment plan: 'All reasonable steps' evidence — willingness to pay

- **ID:** `reasonable-steps-willingness`
- **Idea:** `reasonable-steps`
- **Created:** 2026-09-21
- **Status:** proposed
- **Approval:** required (human owner)

## Central assumption

UK SME employers with 10-100 staff and no dedicated HR system will pay at least £12/month
for a low-cost "all reasonable steps" evidence tool rather than rely on free Acas/EHRC
templates and the HR software they already have.

## Hypothesis

If ten such employers are shown a concrete "reasonable steps" evidence pack — risk
assessment, incident log, policy checklist and training records — and then asked to
commit at £12/month, then at least three will commit, because the 30 October 2026 duty
creates a live compliance need that free guidance alone does not operationalise. This is
stated so that it can be wrong: the record-keeping value may be seen as an HR-software or
legal-advice matter, not a standalone tool.

## Test design

- **Population / sample:** 10 UK employers with 10-100 staff that do not use a dedicated
  HR platform (owner or office/HR manager as the respondent). Not the idea's own network;
  if the sample is network-sourced it is noted and discounted.
- **What participants see or do:** a short walkthrough (screen or PDF) of a prototype
  "reasonable steps" evidence pack: risk assessment template, incident log, policy and
  training-record checklist, and a monthly summary view. Then a direct price question at
  £12/month, £20/month, and a commitment ask.
- **Recruitment route:** at least one non-network channel (e.g. a targeted post or
  professional-network referral), recorded per conversation.
- **Instrumentation / what is recorded:** for each employer — role, staff count, current
  HR tooling, whether they currently document any harassment-prevention steps, reaction
  to the pack, stated willingness at each price, and any concrete commitment (written or
  verbal) to pay. Verbatim quotes captured.
- **Timebox:** 7 calendar days, no more than 10 human hours.
- **What is deliberately NOT included:** no build beyond a throwaway prototype, no legal
  advice, no process serving of real incidents, no outbound contact until approval is
  recorded, no paid advertising, no account creation.

## Decision rule (fixed before running)

| Outcome | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| >=3 of 10 commit at >=£12/mo AND >=1 non-network channel yields >=5 conversations | proceed | advance to a build/positioning decision with the human owner |
| 1-2 commits, or price settles consistently at <=£8/mo | iterate | re-test price/packaging once before deciding |
| 0 commits, or dominant reaction is that free guidance/HR software is enough | stop | record as killed/abandoned and do not build |

## Kill condition

Zero of ten employers make any concrete commitment, or a majority say free Acas/EHRC
guidance and existing HR software already suffice, or legal-advice concerns block the
proposition. Any of these means stop.

## Cost bound

- Money: £20
- Human hours: 10
- Calendar days: 7

## Risks and safeguards

- **No external action before approval.** No contact, spend, publication or account
  creation until `approval.granted = true` is recorded by the human owner.
- **Legal/regulatory sensitivity.** The underlying duty concerns harassment prevention;
  the experiment must not collect real incident data, must not present the prototype as
  legal advice, and should carry a clear "not legal advice" caveat. This is review
  trigger 4 (material legal/regulatory/trust risk) and should be assessed by the human
  owner before external testing.
- **Small-sample risk.** 10 conversations can be unrepresentative; the decision rule is
  deliberately about commitment, not stated interest.
- **No vendor claims as evidence.** Any incumbent check is recorded as secondary
  evidence only.
