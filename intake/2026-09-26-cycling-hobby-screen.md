# Owner-nominated intake: cycling and e-bike ownership (26 September 2026)

- **Recorded:** 2026-09-26
- **Source:** Owner-requested exploration of consumer hobbies whose participants may have relatively high disposable income; cycling was the suggested starting point.
- **Status:** Unvalidated discovery input. This is not an observation pool, idea-ledger entry, candidate, scorecard, or evidence of software demand. It confers no score, evidence level, hard-filter result, or exemption from normal-run triage and source-class rules.
- **Scope:** Desk research only. No cyclist, buyer, retailer, club, manufacturer or app developer was contacted; no test, purchase, spending or public commitment was made.

## Affluence and spending signal

- Sport England Active Lives Online labels its survey “Social status” grouping as its main measure of affluence. In the public query [Participation in the last 28 days by Cycling for leisure and sport — Social status](https://activelives.sportengland.org/Result?queryId=173471), the Nov 2024–25 table reports the measure for 16.2% of NS-SEC 1–2 respondents (managerial/administrative/professional occupations) and 9.1% of NS-SEC 6–8 respondents (lower supervisory/technical, semi-routine/routine, long-term unemployed/never worked), about a 1.78× difference. “Participation in the last 28 days” means at least two moderate-intensity sessions totalling at least 60 minutes. The same direction appears in each displayed wave from 2016–17 through 2024–25.
- This is an occupational-class association with participation, **not** a measure of individual or household disposable income, spending within cycling, or willingness to buy software. The displayed respondent bases (87,141 and 19,535) are survey bases, not population or customer counts. The broad “cycling for leisure and sport” category includes mountain biking, BMX, road, track, cyclo-cross, leisure cycling, exercise bikes and cycle classes. Survey design, self-reporting and non-response limit any inference about affluent enthusiasts or high-end bike owners.
- Cycling UK lists individual membership at £4.84/month and household membership at £7.75/month; British Cycling lists Core at £52/year and Premium at £82/year. These first-party prices show existing paid membership/insurance categories, not participants’ wealth or demand for a new product. Their member-count claims are organization-reported.
- [Query information](https://activelives.sportengland.org/Result/Information?queryId=173471) identifies the activity-category inclusions and frequency/intensity definition used above.

## Repeated maintenance workflows and competition

- A direct Cycling UK Forum thread, [“Maintenance spreadsheet?”](https://forum.cyclinguk.org/viewtopic.php?t=149744) (13–15 February 2022), includes cyclists discussing notes/spreadsheets and component mileage across multiple bikes. One describes a custom VB6 program importing Garmin GPX to update component life and maintenance schedules; other replies mention tracking wear to avoid unnecessary work and using Strava or an add-on for component mileage/reminders. This is firsthand workaround evidence, but it also shows the generic tracking job was already being served by existing tools at the time. It is self-selected, dated forum evidence, not prevalence or willingness-to-pay.
- A Cycling UK Forum result for “bike maintenance record” includes an April 2026 rider who uses a wired mileage counter to track chain and brake-pad life. A July 2026 e-bike-owner thread, [“Advice please regarding this Cube Bike”](https://forum.cyclinguk.org/search.php?keywords=bike+maintenance+app), includes one owner’s complaint about a £75 600-mile service and a chain replacement by 1,400 miles; a reply describes dealer-set Bosch Flow reminders to check chain wear. These are individual reports and opinions, not proof of broad service distrust or an unserved product gap.
- [ProBikeGarage](https://www.probikegarage.com/) directly sells into the generic maintenance-tracking category: ride-linked bike/component mileage, component changes, service notes and interval alerts. Basic tracking is free; Premium is listed at US$2.99/month or US$24.99/year. Its claim of 160,000 cyclists is vendor-reported. This direct product substantially occupies multi-bike component-life tracking; no dissatisfaction or capability gap is established here.
- Bosch’s [eBike Flow app](https://www.bosch-ebike.com/en/products/ebike-flow-app) offers service information and bike-management features for the Bosch Smart System. [Flow+](https://www.bosch-ebike.com/en/products/flow-plus) lists €4.99/month or €39.99/year and describes digital maintenance records, completed service work, battery-status results and next service dates. It is a brand-specific alternative, not evidence that all motor/battery systems or used-bike transactions are covered. Bosch download and review counts are vendor claims.
- [Bikebook](https://www.bikebook.co.uk/) offers a free-to-use mechanic-search/booking comparison service, including e-bike-qualified workshop filters. This is an existing service-discovery route, not verified uptake or evidence of a battery-health testing offer.

## Narrow question: used e-bike battery condition

- A September 2023 Cycling UK Forum result for [“Used ebike - worth it?”](https://forum.cyclinguk.org/search.php?keywords=ebike+battery+health+used) includes one buyer discussing uncertainty about a used battery’s state of health and wishing for a load test or a long test ride. This is a dated anecdote; frequency and buyer willingness to pay are unknown.
- Bosch’s [battery safety guidance](https://www.bosch-ebike.com/en/service/battery-safety) offers a serial-number check for whether a service case has been registered and directs owners to specialist dealers for inspection. It warns against opening, repairing or reconditioning faulty packs. That serial/service-case check is not documented there as a measured capacity or state-of-health report. Bosch Flow+ does document battery-status results within its own ecosystem, so any cross-brand or resale gap needs direct verification.
- A potentially sharper **research hypothesis**, not a claimed opportunity, is an independently verifiable battery-condition report at used e-bike purchase where existing dealer/OEM results are not portable between brands or transactions. This is narrower than “OEMs do not show battery health”: Bosch documents battery-status results in its system, and the checked UK service offers use brand-specific dealer diagnostics (details below). The payer could be the buyer, seller, marketplace or shop, and is not yet established.

### Follow-up: resale checks and existing service (26 September 2026)

- UK retailer [Cycle Exchange’s refurbished-bike process](https://www.cycleexchange.co.uk/blogs/news/our-refurb-process-explained) (published 10 November 2025) says its e-bike batteries are tested for capacity, charge cycles and overall health; it backs refurbished bikes with a 12-month warranty. This shows that battery diagnostics already form part of a retailer’s pre-sale assurance offer. The page does not disclose a test protocol, report format, standalone price, transaction volume or whether a transferable report accompanies each advertised bike. The service is bundled with the retailer’s own refurbishment, not evidenced as an independent report available to private sellers/buyers.
- The same retailer lists a [second-hand electric-bike category](https://www.cycleexchange.co.uk/collections/electric-bikes). Its general sale process describes bikes as serviced and warrantied, but the inspected category page exposed no individual inventory to verify how often battery results appear in listings.
- International used-bike marketplace [buycycle’s seller guide](https://buycycle.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/20385308791569-create-the-perfect-bike-listing) asks sellers to specify condition, mileage and service history, and requires a charger with e-bike listings; it does not describe a structured battery-health field or mandatory battery test. The platform advertises buyer protection and a 48-hour inspection window, but this screen did not establish UK availability or whether battery checks are handled elsewhere in its flow.
- UK service businesses already list paid battery diagnostics:
  - [Cycle Maintenance UK](https://cyclemaintenanceuk.com/servicing) lists a £99 standard service with a £35 e-bike diagnostic add-on (£124 total), or a £149 full service including e-bike tests. The package describes use of official Bosch/Mahle/Shimano dealer software and a battery-health/cell-condition report. Coverage is limited to Kent/South East London, and the offer says other systems cannot use the official diagnostic software.
  - [All4Ebikes](https://all4ebikes.co.uk/service-and-repair/battery-repairs-service/e-bike-battery-diagnostic-service/) lists a £20 diagnostic (some voltage options cost £5 extra), covering cell balance, voltage, capacity, BMS function and load behavior with a report/recommendations. It asks customers to send photos and await acceptance before mailing the battery; the page showed no reviews when checked.
  - [eMovement](https://emovement.co.uk/pages/bosch-ebike-partner), a Bosch Certified Partner, says it uses a Bosch Capacity Tester to report remaining capacity in Wh and records charge cycles, temperature and deep discharges, framing the report as useful for resale. No price is published.
- The checked Bosch material documents battery-status results and remaining-capacity testing within its own system. It does not establish what is available across other brands or whether reports transfer between systems or transactions. Bosch itself warns against opening, repairing or reconditioning faulty packs. A universal report could therefore be technically and safety-sensitive; this source screen did not establish a common cross-brand test protocol or transferable report standard.
- This counterevidence substantially weakens the idea that used e-bike buyers lack a route to battery testing: UK providers list paid diagnostics, refurbishers bundle tests into warranties, and Bosch exposes battery status within its own system. A narrow private-sale portability gap remains unproven. The listed prices establish offers only—not test volume, transaction frequency, demand for independent certification, willingness to pay, or whether reports are accepted by a buyer or marketplace.

## Disposition and open questions

Generic multi-bike maintenance logging is already served by consumer apps, community workarounds and brand ecosystems; do not promote it as a novel product. **Lean drop the broad used e-bike battery-report framing as an opportunity lead:** paid diagnostic services, brand-specific information and refurbisher assurance already exist, while this screen found no evidence of a repeated private-sale buyer gap or demand for a transferable report. Reopen only if further evidence establishes frequent unmet buyer need plus a feasible, non-destructive, cross-brand method and an accessible payer/distribution route. This is a desk-research disposition, not a commercial kill, idea-ledger change or score. No direct high-income or spend-by-hobby statistic was established, and the Active Lives proxy does not establish those claims.

This remains owner-nominated intake, not an idea, seed, candidate, experiment or validation-ready proposal. Any buyer contact, measurement access or test would require owner approval. No such action occurred in this screen.
