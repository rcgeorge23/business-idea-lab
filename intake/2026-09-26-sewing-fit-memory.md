# Owner-nominated intake: consumer sewing fit-memory (26 September 2026)

- **Recorded:** 2026-09-26
- **Source:** Owner-selected consumer/hobby discovery lead, refined from the “sewing fit passport” hypothesis.
- **Status:** Unvalidated discovery input. This is not an idea-ledger entry, candidate, scorecard, or evidence of demand. It confers no score, evidence level, hard-filter result, or exemption from a future normal run's observation/source-class rules.
- **Scope:** Desk research only. No sewist, teacher, pattern maker or retailer was contacted; no product, test, spending or public commitment is authorized by this note.

## Why this intake exists

The broad hypothesis was a digital sewing “fit passport”: help a home sewist remember fit choices and adjustments between garment projects, potentially across pattern brands. The user's preferred consumer/hobby direction is retained for possible inclusion in a later normal observation sweep. Fresh desk research materially weakens the storage/remembering version. A possible cross-brand translation layer remains only an unverified mechanism, not a candidate.

## H1 — Cross-brand personal fit history or adjustment translation

- **Buyer / user hypothesis:** A home sewist who makes garments from multiple pattern companies is both user and possible consumer payer. Frequency of cross-brand sewing, authority to pay, willingness to pay, and reachable segment are unknown.
- **Observed behavior:** Two public PatternReview discussions show sewists working through multiple body/pattern fit adjustments and muslins. In the April 2026 discussion, one experienced participant recommends making a well-fitting sloper and using it to guide later patterns. This is direct evidence that fit diagnosis can require effort and peer advice; two threads do not establish prevalence, severity, or payment.
- **Important correction:** PatternReview review 208126 describes a sewist making a third version of one pattern and notes that the jacket had been lengthened on earlier versions. The author explicitly says the current version had no fit alterations. It is not evidence that an earlier adjustment was reused on that version.
- **Inference, not observation:** Remembering body/pattern-specific outcomes might reduce repeat note-searching or fitting work. No measured time, fabric savings, fit outcome, or willingness-to-pay evidence was found.
- **Potential mechanism:** Store dated body-measurement snapshots and pattern-specific fit outcomes, or translate a proven adjustment to another pattern block. The storage mechanism is already occupied; cross-brand translation is unverified and may be technically unsound because pattern blocks and fitting causes differ.

## Alternatives and counterevidence

- **Threadloop** has free pattern/project organization. Its first-party guides document project-level pattern size, fit, use and adjustments; personal pattern notes; and measurement history saved under timestamps. It states that a shared project's body measurements can reflect the measurements at completion. Threadloop Plus is listed at US$5/month or US$48/year, but the cited fit-history pages do not establish that users pay specifically for this feature.
- **Sewbench** is listed as a free, offline sewing tracker with project records for pattern, fabric, size cut, modifications, photos and notes, including annotations on pattern PDFs. It is an early/TestFlight-beta offer; adoption is unknown.
- **Stash Hub** offers pattern, measurement and project organization. A November 2025 support thread records a user asking to preserve measurements from prior projects; the developer replied that updating measurements then updated all projects and said a fix was on the roadmap. The current public pages do not establish whether that roadmap item shipped. Threadloop already documents timestamped measurement history.
- **BeeSilva's physical *My Sewing Journal*** is offered for £20.99 and provides guided measurement/fit logs for up to 115 projects. Its product page shows 22 reviews and seller-selected/verified-buyer testimonials about recalling alterations and prior project details. This is a paid substitute and a signal that the journaling job is recognized; the page does not independently verify sales volume or willingness to pay for software.
- **PatternReview** supplies free community, discussion and project/pattern-review records and sells fitting classes (one listed at US$49). Its community recommends slopers as a reusable manual fitting method. Paper notes and pattern annotations are also substitutes.
- Other sewing organizers (including Stash Hub, Pleats and Haberdasheroo) already address general inventory, measurements, patterns or project records. A new stash/project organizer is not the proposed wedge.

**Strongest counterargument:** The “passport” stores information that several free or low-cost apps, journals and community practices already capture. The potentially novel claim—software that safely translates adjustments between pattern brands—has no observed buyer request or demonstrated reliability; a sloper or made-to-measure pattern may solve the job instead. On present evidence there is no clear economic buyer or defensible product wedge.

## Disposition and critical unknowns

- Do not create or score an idea from this intake. Generic sewing fit logging is already served; the cross-brand translation hypothesis is not validated and may fail on feasibility as well as demand.
- Unknown: how often high-frequency sewists lose/recreate fitting knowledge despite existing digital and paper tools; whether adjustments transfer between blocks; what the named competitors currently support in practice; whether any reachable segment pays for a better outcome; and whether the owner has unusual sewing-community access or relevant expertise.
- The prior PatternReview threads are self-selected, qualitative examples. Vendor descriptions, pricing, review counts and testimonials establish offers/claims, not independent product adoption, outcomes or addressable demand.
- **Proposed disconfirming test (not approved or run):** after confirming current competitors, seek owner approval for a small, privacy-safe test with frequent multi-brand garment sewists. Ask them to reconstruct recent fit decisions using their existing journals/apps/slopers, then test whether a specific cross-pattern translation produces a result they trust and would pay for. Stop if current tools or slopers adequately solve the job, if translation is judged unreliable, or if participants will not make a concrete payment commitment. No contact or child/private-data access is authorized here.
