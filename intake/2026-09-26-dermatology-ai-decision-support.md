# Owner-nominated intake: AI-assisted dermatology decision support (26 September 2026)

- **Recorded:** 2026-09-26
- **Source:** Owner-proposed concept: capture a dermatology consultant's knowledge, potentially in a vector database, and let other medical professionals use AI to help assess rashes, moles or other skin issues.
- **Status:** Unvalidated discovery input only. Not an idea-ledger entry, candidate, scorecard, clinical recommendation, or evidence of demand. It confers no score, evidence level or hard-filter result.
- **Scope:** Desk research only. No clinician or patient was contacted; no patient records/images were accessed; no diagnosis, clinical test, external deployment, spending or public commitment occurred.

## Frame the job before the technology

- “Assess a mole / possible skin cancer” and “differentiate an inflammatory rash” are separate clinical jobs. They can involve different users, pathways, reference standards, risks and outcomes. A broad tool for “skin issues” does not yet identify a buyer or a decision to improve.
- A vector/RAG store can retrieve and cite consultant-authored guidance or cases. It does not by itself train a visual-recognition model, establish diagnostic accuracy, or validate a differential. If the product interprets images or provides patient-specific diagnostic recommendations, it requires evidence for that intended use; calling it an expert system or adding a disclaimer does not establish safety or remove applicable regulation.
- A single consultant's expertise may help author a focused knowledge resource, but does not demonstrate that recommendations generalize across clinicians, settings, skin tones, rare presentations or conflicting conditions. Rights to use any existing patient images/records and the consultant's reusable IP are unknown.

## Existing clinical pathway and products

- **Direct pathway incumbent:** NICE [HTG746](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg746/chapter/1-Recommendations), updated 2026-03-17, conditionally supports Skin Analytics' DERM in the urgent suspected skin-cancer teledermatology pathway during a three-year evidence-generation period, subject to appropriate regulatory approval and NHS DTAC approval. This is not general rash diagnosis. The NICE [technology description](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg746/chapter/2-The-technologies) lists £30 per referral, £8.20 for staff review, a £35.90 discounted option if biopsy results are shared, and £17 for an additional company dermatologist read. These are published offer/contract prices, not independent sales or adoption evidence.
- NICE requires continued evidence and monitoring, including accuracy across skin tones (especially darker skin) and effects on referrals, appointments, biopsies and capacity. Its [committee discussion](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg746/chapter/3-Committee-discussion) describes uncertainty in comparative cost-effectiveness and evidence limitations. The pathway shows that a buyer can fund a specific lesion-triage service; it does not show that a separate text/vector knowledge assistant has a budget.
- NICE [MIB311](https://www.nice.org.uk/advice/mib311/chapter/The-technology) describes FotoFinder Moleanalyzer pro as a dermatologist-selected biopsy adjunct, not an autonomous diagnosis. The report lists single-user prices of £1,210/year for an AI scoring assistant or £1,750/year for an offline package, plus mapping hardware; these are report-era prices, not verified current quotes or evidence of uptake.
- Vendor pages also market products closer to expert-authored or broad diagnostic support: [ALCROEN](https://alcroen.com/) describes informational ranked differentials and pathways based on a clinician's knowledge; [Autoderm](https://autoderm.ai/virtual-primary-care/) claims coverage of 70+ conditions and deployments; [DermaSetuAI](https://www.dermasetuai.com/) claims 114+ conditions and an NHS deployment; [Dermatic](https://dermatic.health/) markets explainable AI. These are vendor descriptions/claims, not independently verified clinical performance, procurement or adoption. Their existence weakens novelty of the broad concept without establishing that their products work or meet every need.
- Established references include [DermNet NZ](https://dermnetnz.org/), with dermatologist-reviewed educational content and a diagnostic aid, [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/) primary-care guidance, and [NICE CKS](https://cks.nice.org.uk/). A 2018 [BMJ announcement](https://www.bmj.com/company/newsroom/health-education-england/) reported BMJ Best Practice had been procured for NHS staff then; current entitlement was not verified in this screen. A reference-only product would need to solve a documented workflow problem beyond making existing guidance searchable.
- NHS procurement examples include DERM's [direct-award notice](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/039764-2025), [Consultant Connect](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/000107-2025) at a listed £42.50/referral, and a [community teledermatology contract](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/026272-2024/PDF). They indicate spending on pathway capacity/services, not a budget for this proposed product. NICE specifically notes that whether DERM frees capacity for non-cancer inflammatory conditions remains uncertain; this is a research question, not proof of unmet demand.

## Focused primary-care inflammatory-rash screen (26 September 2026)

- **Tested workflow:** primary-care assessment of a possible inflammatory rash, using atopic eczema as a concrete example. This was chosen to challenge the broad idea, not because an eczema AI opportunity is established.
- The Primary Care Dermatology Society’s [Cunliffe General Dermatology Diagnostic Tool](https://www.pcds.org.uk/general-dermatology-table) explicitly describes itself as a visual diagnostic tool for inflammatory skin conditions and other rashes. It organizes differentials by history, predominant site and morphology, including darker-skin presentations, and links to condition guidance and adult/paediatric eczema pathways. This is a directly accessible non-AI alternative for the proposed diagnostic-reference job; it does not prove clinician use, satisfaction or outcomes.
- [NICE CKS](https://cks.nice.org.uk/about) says it provides concise answers to common primary-care clinical questions across 370+ topics, written by an expert multidisciplinary team and supported by specialist reviewers. It is commissioned and funded by NICE, but is not itself equivalent to formal NICE guidance; content is copyrighted by Agilio and reuse is governed by its licence. This makes “put a consultant’s knowledge into a searchable assistant” a weak wedge by itself, and any commercial reuse of CKS material would need rights checked.
- The NHS [e-RS advice-and-guidance workflow](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/e-referral-service/document-library/advice-and-guidance-toolkit/advice-and-guidance-for-gps-and-referring-clinician-teams) lets a GP send clinical information and, where appropriate, images for a provider clinician to respond with diagnosis/management advice or request more information. NHS Digital says A&G should generally not be used for questions where accessible local or national guidance already exists. This is an existing human-specialist path for residual uncertainty, not evidence that a new AI tool is funded or would be preferred.
- NHS England’s [2025/26 GP A&G financial specification](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/update-to-the-gp-contract-agreements-2025-26-financial-implications) described up to £80m nationally and £20 per eligible pre-referral request for that year’s enhanced service. This was not dermatology-specific and paid for the human A&G workflow; the cited specification covers 2025/26 only, and continuation into 2026/27 was not verified. It is therefore historical adjacent spending, not a current AI budget. No public purchasing line for inflammatory-rash differential AI was identified in this focused screen.
- A 2026 cross-sectional study of 246 Palestinian physicians reported limited formal dermatology training and mostly self-rated average diagnostic confidence ([Yacoub et al., BMC Primary Care](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13591905)). It is non-UK, convenience-sampled and measures confidence rather than diagnostic accuracy; it does not establish UK clinician need, product impact or willingness to pay.

**Screen result:** the differential/reference layer for common inflammatory rashes is already substantially covered by accessible PCDS and NICE CKS material, while questions beyond available guidance have a human A&G route. This does not show clinicians are satisfied or prove an AI could not improve a specific decision. It does mean the missing evidence is a measured UK decision failure, not a lack of consultant knowledge in a vector database. The 2025/26 A&G payment shows adjacent human-service spending only; it does not transfer to an AI product.

## Evidence, governance and feasibility constraints

- The [MHRA software/AI-as-a-medical-device guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device) and [standalone-software guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-software-applications-apps) make intended purpose central to qualification and classification. A product explicitly intended to help clinicians diagnose may fall within software-as-a-medical-device requirements; exact qualification/classification depends on the design, outputs and intended use and has not been determined here. MHRA's [intended-purpose guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crafting-an-intended-purpose-in-the-context-of-software-as-a-medical-device-samd) says to specify what the product does, users, beneficiaries and setting.
- NICE's [Evidence Standards Framework](https://www.nice.org.uk/corporate/ecd7) includes effectiveness and real-world evidence, performance across population subgroups, reliable health information, professional oversight, comparison with current care, resource/budget impact, deployment, training and ongoing monitoring. NHS clinical-safety standards [DCB0129/DCB0160](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/clinical-safety/applicability-of-dcb-0129-and-dcb-0160) apply to relevant manufacturers and deploying organisations.
- The [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data/) treats health information, including diagnoses and images revealing health status, as special-category data. Lawful use, permissions, security and controller/processor roles for any patient examples or images remain unresolved. No consultant's access rights or consent to reuse existing records were verified.
- Before an image-diagnosis claim, a credible evidence plan would need a narrowly specified task, a clinically defensible reference standard, representative multi-site cases and skin tones, independent validation, safe uncertainty/abstention behavior, subgroup/harm analysis and monitoring. Consultant prose alone is not a substitute for these. This is an inference from the intended use and evidence requirements, not a completed product design.

## Buyer, gap and next research gate

- Possible users include GPs, nurses, urgent-care clinicians, dermatologists or teledermatology staff; the product's current “other medical professionals” audience is too broad. Possible buyers could be an NHS trust/commissioner or a provider, but no named buyer, budget owner or purchasing evidence for inflammatory-rash decision support was identified.
- The remaining question is whether a specific non-cancer workflow has a persistent, measurable failure after current guidance and referral advice are considered, and whether a reachable buyer funds improvement. The atopic-eczema primary-care screen above found close alternatives and no AI-specific budget, so the gap remains unproven and the broad concept should not advance on the current evidence.
- **Next gate before further product design:** only continue if the owner can identify a specific clinical setting and decision where current PCDS/CKS guidance and local A&G do not resolve the problem. A further desk check could map one ICB’s public pathway/procurement; it would not prove clinical safety or willingness to pay. Any clinician interviews, patient-data access or clinical evaluation need owner permission and appropriate experiment/safety/governance review first.

## Disposition

**Lean-reject the broad “train one consultant's knowledge into a vector database to diagnose skin issues” framing.** The exact task, intended purpose, valid data rights, clinical evidence, regulatory route, buyer and budget are all unresolved; close products and free references exist. Preserve only the narrower inflammatory-rash workflow question as unvalidated intake, not an idea, seed, score or experiment. No external action or clinical use occurred.
