## Outcome

**Zero promotions.** The avenue produced mainly crowded, incumbent-owned or
low-budget observations. Principal findings:

- **MIS migration data integrity (O1, O9, O10, O18):** a real, dated, recurring
  operational seam — Arbor's own onboarding guides document a 12-week process with
  data cleansing, baseline Data Counts, post-migration checks and court orders
  that do not migrate — but it is owned end-to-end by the incoming MIS and by an
  established paid consultancy market (School ICT, School Business Services, MIS
  Data Solutions, Involve Education). One-shot economics.
- **Attendance data sharing (O2):** Wonde middleware occupies the extraction
  layer; MIS vendors are absorbing the feed natively.
- **CTF 26 / in-year transfers (O3):** CTF export is a standard MIS feature; LA
  forms are free and LA-owned.
- **Safeguarding (O4):** CPOMS and MyConcern dominate, both MIS-integrated.
- **Clubs/trips/events (O5):** at least five credible products including MIS-native
  modules.
- **Teacher workload (O6, O16):** DfE's evidence base is strong and dated, but the
  market response is large and AI-heavy, and the residual problem is institutional
  practice rather than a missing tool.
- **School finance (O7):** ParentPay, PS Financials, Sage and Bromcom Finance own
  the reconciliation workflow.
- **MIS procurement (O8):** a real change (DfE framework from 2027, 3-year deal
  cap, data-portability requirements) but the buyer is the DfE/LA, not a school.
- **Independent schools (O12, O17):** the integrated-platform pitch is exactly the
  problem, sold by multiple credible vendors; O17 is the prior rejection O16
  re-recorded with its original reason preserved.
- **Pupil-facing learning (O14):** >1,000 EdTech suppliers; the binding constraint
  is proving learning impact, which a small entrant cannot do cheaply.
