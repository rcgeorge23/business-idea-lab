## Observation

The WasteTrack kill found the domestic receiver-side digital waste tracking market
already saturated (54-79 Defra-approved providers, free tiers, bundled weighbridge and
waste-management systems). The same research surfaced a materially different, deferred
problem:

> The EU's Digital Waste Shipment System (DIWASS), under Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 and
> Implementing Regulation 2025/1290, became the mandatory route for transboundary waste
> shipments from 21 May 2026, replacing paper Annex VII/IB/IA documentation. Reported
> adoption was only ~18.1% of ~137,000 eligible operators. UK green-list (Article 18)
> waste tracking was explicitly deferred to a future phase of UK digital waste tracking,
> and waste carrier/broker/dealer registration becomes a permit-based system from
> 22 July 2027.

## Why this is materially different from the parent

- **Different job:** cross-border shipment notification and documentation (Annex VII /
  DIWASS), not domestic receiver record-keeping.
- **Different buyer:** UK waste exporters, brokers and customs/forwarding intermediaries,
  not permitted UK receiving-site operators.
- **Different regime and competitive set:** EU DIWASS compliance tooling and
  freight/customs software, not the Defra-approved domestic provider list.
- **Non-inheriting:** the parent's score (58.9), its `defensible_wedge` fail and its
  `Plausible` evidence level are irrelevant here; this seed starts at `unexplored` with
  no score.

## What a later run should check first

1. Are UK exporters/brokers of green-list waste actually exposed to DIWASS, or does the
   notification duty fall on the EU consignee/competent authority?
2. Is there a dated, felt discontinuity on the UK side (e.g. the planned UK DWT
   green-list phase, or the 22 July 2027 permit reform) rather than only an EU benefit?
3. Is there a defensible wedge against DIWASS compliance vendors and customs/freight
   software, or is this a service an existing forwarder already performs?
