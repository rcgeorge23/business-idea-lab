## Observation

From 6 April 2027 the grantee of an option, conditional contract, pre-emption right
or promotion right over registered land must send prescribed information to HM Land
Registry within 60 days of grant, assignment or variation, submitted digitally by an
individually regulated conveyancer through a new HMLR service (The Provision of
Information (Contractual Control) (Registered Land) Regulations 2026, SI 2026/615,
in force 6 Apr 2027). HMLR must then publish a dataset of these contractual-control
agreements "as soon as possible" after 6 April 2028, updated monthly. The rejection
was correct for a submission-workflow product: HMLR owns the channel and the duty is
pre-launch. The adjacent, different opportunity is what becomes possible once HMLR
publishes the dataset.

Sources: GOV.UK guidance 2026-03-09; GOV.UK news 2026-06-12; SI 2026/615 on
legislation.gov.uk; Simmons & Simmons 2026-03-24 (all cited in
`observations/20260922T060326Z-normal.md`, O10).

## Why it is materially different from the rejected observation

The rejected observation was a conveyancer-facing submission/exception workflow
competing with a channel HMLR is building itself. This seed is a data-intelligence
product over a dataset that does not yet exist publicly: it would serve developers,
land promoters, site finders, lenders, local-authority regeneration teams and
investors who want to see where land is tied up by options, promotion agreements and
conditional contracts. That is a different buyer, a different mechanism (search and
monitoring over published data rather than a filing workflow) and a different
competitive position (the filing incumbents are not data publishers).

## What a later run should check first

- Whether HMLR has confirmed the dataset's fields, licence, update cadence and any
  bulk/API access, and whether it is likely to be published on time.
- Whether any commercial provider already publishes or resells an equivalent
  land-control/option/promotion dataset (check the established property-data
  vendors before assuming an unoccupied seam).
- Whether the affected buyers (promoters, site finders, lenders) already assemble
  this from Planning Data, local-authority registers, Land Registry title data and
  their own deal pipelines, and whether they would pay for a consolidated view.
- Whether the 60-day filing duty itself creates a usable derived signal (for
  example, detecting agreements before the dataset is published).
