## Observation

The PRS Database Regulations 2026 place a per-property registration, update (within 28 days) and
annual renewal duty on the person making a dwelling entry, with a fee payable before the entry. The
regime rolls out region by region from 15 December 2026 (West Midlands first) to all eligible
England properties by 14 November 2027. The visible supply response so far targets letting agents and
portfolios: a £29/month flat "unlimited properties" PRS compliance platform with CSV import, and a
major lettings platform (Goodlord) publicly stating it is "looking at how we can support that
process" and integrating with agency CRMs (Reapit, Street, Alto) that already hold compliance data.
Nothing found in this run targets the individual self-managing landlord who owns one or a few
properties and must perform the same per-property duty personally.

## Why it is materially different from the parent

`prsregister` and the seed `prs-listing-precheck` both sell into the professional channel - agents,
portals and CRM vendors - and were contestable as a feature of lettings platforms and agent CRMs.
This observation has a different buyer (individual self-managing landlords, consumer not B2B), a
different mechanism (a consumer subscription that registers, updates and renews each property and
tracks the numbers ahead of marketing), and a different competitive position (agent/platform tooling
does not reach landlords who do not use an agent). It also addresses the recorded kill reason for the
parent rather than restating it.

## What a later run should check first

1. Do individual self-managing landlords already get adequate, free help from the GOV.UK service and
   existing landlord software (for example Landlord Vision, Hammock), such that the job is already
   covered? Treat any such incumbent as a direct competitor selling the proposed position.
2. Is there a dated, evidenced sign that individual landlords will pay a subscription rather than do
   a once-a-year task themselves, and what price clears the low willingness to pay?
3. How would a consumer service reach these landlords without paid acquisition (search/content,
   landlord associations, letting-agent referrals), and is that channel durable after the initial
   registration wave ends?
4. Does the consumer framing avoid the same "feature of a category" trap - i.e. is the wedge
   genuinely different from landlord accounting/portfolio software bundling PRS registration?
