## Observation

The GrantScout adversarial pass found that grant *discovery* is not a viable wedge:
GOV.UK "Find a grant" provides authoritative free discovery, and GovOwed already sells
the flat-fee matching wedge. During that review the following observation was recorded:

> The value may lie in application **quality and win rate** rather than discovery —
> SMEs and their advisers struggle to write competitive applications, and the money at
> stake per successful bid is large relative to any software or service fee.

## Why this is materially different from the parent

- **Different job:** winning a specific bid (outcome), not receiving a list of schemes.
- **Different buyer and timing:** a business with a live application deadline, or the
  accountant/consultant already paid to help; the parent targeted passive monthly
  digest subscribers.
- **Different pricing logic:** linked to outcome/effort (per-application or success-
  linked), not a £19/month subscription.
- **Different competitive set:** bid-writing consultancies, not grant-discovery tools.
- **Non-inheriting:** the parent's score (55.8), its `Promising` evidence level and its
  kill reason are all irrelevant to this seed; it starts at `unexplored` with no score.

## What a later run should check first

1. Is there evidence SMEs (or their advisers) actually pay for application help today,
   and at what price and volume? (Consultancy pricing, tender outcomes, accountant
   service lists.)
2. Is there a recent discontinuity (why now?) — e.g. a new scheme with high volume and
   simple eligibility, a new public dataset, or an LLM capability that materially cuts
   the cost of drafting a competitive application.
3. Does a defensible wedge exist against incumbent bid-writing services and free
   government guidance, or is this feature-of-category territory?
