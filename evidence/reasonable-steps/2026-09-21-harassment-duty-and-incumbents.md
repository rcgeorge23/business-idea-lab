# Evidence: the 30 October 2026 "all reasonable steps" duty and the current provider landscape

- **Idea / scope:** `reasonable-steps`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** Employment Rights Act 2025, legislation.gov.uk (ukpga/2025/36, ss.20-24, s.159(3)); CIPD harassment-law changes briefing; Acas "Harassment law changes" (acas.org.uk/employment-rights-act-2025/harassment-law-changes); business.gov.uk "Employment Rights Act" campaign page; hilldickinson.com; lewissilkin.com (2026-09-17)
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-21
- **Credibility:** primary legislation plus official/authoritative guidance (Acas, gov.uk). Strong for what the law requires and when. Weak for employer willingness to pay, which no source here establishes.

## Claim(s) supported

- A specific, dated legal change: from 30 October 2026 employers must take "all reasonable steps" to prevent sexual harassment, and must not permit third-party harassment, with liability unless "all reasonable steps" were taken.
- The statute contemplates a concrete, recordable set of steps (risk assessment, monitoring/recording incidents, policy, training, handling complaints), which is the basis for an evidence-of-compliance product.
- Authoritative free guidance (Acas/EHRC) and paid training already exist; the market is fragmented across HR platforms, legal advisers and training providers; no dedicated low-cost SME evidence tool was found in this search.

## Exact detail

- Employment Rights Act 2025 (c.36, Royal Assent 18 December 2025): s.20 amends Equality Act 2010 s.40A to require "all" (not merely "reasonable") steps to prevent sexual harassment; s.21 introduces a third-party harassment duty (an employer must not permit it and is liable unless it took all reasonable steps); s.22 gives power to specify by regulations what counts (risk assessments, plans/policies, reporting, handling complaints); ss.23-24 concern protected disclosures/NDAs. Commencement is by regulations under s.159(3); the 30 October 2026 date is as briefed by CIPD and Acas.
- Acas (acas.org.uk/employment-rights-act-2025/harassment-law-changes) lists third parties including customers, clients, service users, contractors, conference attendees and the public, and suggested steps including risk assessments, monitoring/recording incidents, a policy, training (including de-escalation), contract/T&C updates, signage, staffing, equipment and cameras. It notes "all reasonable steps" means what is possible with the employer's resources, and gives worked examples.
- business.gov.uk states that the Worker Protection Act 2023 duty (from October 2024) required "reasonable steps" and that the Employment Rights Act 2025 strengthens this to "all reasonable steps" plus the third-party duty from 30 October 2026; employer guidance is to be published in advance. It points to EHRC technical guidance and to Acas paid courses/training (fees apply). The changes apply in England, Wales and Scotland, not Northern Ireland.
- Law-firm commentary (hilldickinson.com; lewissilkin.com, 2026-09-17) confirms the same commencement date and recommends that employers document their steps in advance.
- Search for dedicated low-cost tooling found none: the observed alternatives are free official guidance, paid generic training, and generalist HR software (which the searched pages did not evidence as offering a dedicated third-party-harassment/evidence workflow). This is a gap in the searched sources, not proof the gap is unserved.

## Why it is credible

The legal change and its date come from primary legislation and official/authoritative channels (Acas, gov.uk). The incumbency picture is drawn from the same official guidance plus practitioner commentary.

## What it does NOT show

- Does not show that UK SMEs will pay for a dedicated tool rather than rely on free Acas/EHRC templates or existing HR software.
- Does not prove no competitor tool exists — only that none was surfaced by these searches.
- Does not quantify the size of the affected SME population or their current spend; those remain assumptions to be tested.
