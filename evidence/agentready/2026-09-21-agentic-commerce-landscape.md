# Evidence: agentic-commerce platforms, protocols and the bundled-integration position

- **Idea / scope:** `agentready`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** Shopify UCP pages (shopify.com, 2026-09-18); Shopify blog "Agentic Storefronts" (2026-04-02); Shopify blog "How agentic commerce works" (2026-04-30); WooCommerce news "Stripe Agentic Commerce Suite" (2025-12-11); WooCommerce agentic-commerce product page (2026-08-06); developers.openai.com/commerce (updated 2026-09-20); openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt; Stripe newsroom (2025-09-29); PayPal newsroom (2025-10-28); agenticcommerce.dev; agenticcommerceprotocol.info/observatory (2026-09-01); agenticplug.ai protocol survey (2026-09-19); ekamoira.com ACP guide
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-21 (sources carry their own publication/update dates above)
- **Credibility:** primary platform documentation plus vendor announcement pages. Strong for facts about what platforms have shipped and what they charge; weak for merchant adoption or willingness to pay, which none of these establish.

## Claim(s) supported

- A new agent-mediated buying surface opened between September 2025 and 2026, and the large commerce platforms now bundle readiness for it at no incremental fee.
- The off-platform/independent merchant segment is not served first-party by those bundles, but the protocol vendors and agency ecosystem are already moving on it.
- The proposed "make merchants agent-ready" wedge is not a new capability with defensible ground: it is a feature shipping inside platforms and their payment partners.

## Exact detail

- OpenAI and Stripe launched Instant Checkout on 29 September 2025 (US ChatGPT users buying from US Etsy sellers; Stripe newsroom, 2025-09-29). The Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) was open-sourced under Apache 2.0 at agenticcommerce.dev. PayPal announced ACP adoption on 28 October 2025 "to connect tens of millions of merchants" (PayPal newsroom, 2025-10-28).
- Shopify states it co-developed the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) with Google, an open standard backed by Amazon, Amex, Etsy, Mastercard, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce, Stripe, Target, Walmart and Visa; the protocol edition is dated 2026-08 and the page 2026-09-18.
- Shopify "Agentic Storefronts" (blog, 2026-04-02; product page, 2026-04-30) is described as a sales channel giving merchants access to AI surfaces — ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google AI Mode/Gemini — with "no apps, no custom integrations, no fees beyond standard processing", from March 2026.
- WooCommerce announced Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite on 11 December 2025 as a day-one partner (Stripe handles discovery, checkout, payments, fraud). By 2026-08-06 WooCommerce stated MCP is live (WordPress 6.9 / WooCommerce 10.3, nine abilities) with UCP and ACP "coming soon" via Google/Stripe extensions.
- The ACP observatory (2026-09-01) records integration by Target, Sephora, Nordstrom, Lowe's, Best Buy, Home Depot, Wayfair and Walmart, protocol extension to discovery (2026-03-24) and other protocols (MPP, x402). A protocol survey (agenticplug.ai, 2026-09-19) counts eight active agentic-commerce protocols and lists Shopify, Salesforce Commerce Cloud and BigCommerce supporting ACP/UCP, with WooCommerce in progress.
- OpenAI's merchant documentation (developers.openai.com/commerce, updated 2026-09-20) requires a product feed plus agentic-checkout REST endpoints and a delegated-payment spec; OpenAI is not merchant of record. Fee levels are not consistently documented publicly (a third-party guide cites a percentage fee; another source's launch date conflicts with the OpenAI/Stripe September 2025 launch), so fee claims are treated as unverified.

## Why it is credible

These are first-party platform and payment-provider announcements and product pages, internally consistent on the core fact that agentic checkout went live in late 2025 and that major platforms now absorb readiness into their standard offer.

## What it does NOT show

- Does not show that independent merchants will pay a third party for agent-readiness, nor penetration among off-platform merchants.
- Does not establish the exact transaction fee or that fees are stable; published fee detail is conflicting.
- Does not show demand; it shows supply-side platform action, which is evidence against a standalone wedge rather than for one.
