# Evidence: Non-UK locale regulation, indirect tax and privacy affecting a UK-run auction platform

- **Idea / scope:** `aucly` (locale-expansion assessment)
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** Tax authorities and data-protection regulators, listed per claim below
  with dates.
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-22
- **Credibility:** primary (tax authorities, regulators, legislation) with some
  credible-secondary law-firm summaries where noted.

## Claim(s) supported

**Indirect tax / VAT / GST / sales tax:**

- A non-resident supplying remote services to **New Zealand** customers must
  register for GST once supplies to NZ customers were **NZ$60,000+** in the last 12
  months or are expected in the next 12 months (unless supplies are only to
  GST-registered businesses for business use, which are treated as outside NZ).
  Source: IRD, *Supplying remote services into New Zealand* (page dated
  2026-09-12); IR546 (2026); IS 21/03.
- A non-resident supplier of imported services/digital products to **Australian**
  consumers must register for GST once GST turnover connected with Australia is
  **A$75,000+** (A$150,000 for non-profits); 10% GST; supplies to GST-registered
  business recipients are excluded; simplified registration is available without an
  ABN. Source: ATO pages (dated 2025-09-11); ATO GSTR 2017/1.
- A **UK (non-EU) business cannot use the EU Union One Stop Shop for services**: it
  must use the Non-Union OSS or register in each Member State and must charge the
  **destination-country** VAT rate from the first sale. The usual **EUR 10,000**
  cross-border B2C threshold benefit applies only to suppliers established in a
  single Member State. Ireland's standard VAT rate is 23%. Sources: European
  Commission OSS pages (`vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu`), corroborated by
  OpenAccountants *EU OSS Digital* (validation date April 2026).
- **US sales tax** is state-by-state under post-*Wayfair* economic-nexus rules:
  **41 states use US$100,000**, Alabama and Mississippi US$250,000, and California,
  New York and Texas US$500,000 (New York requires **both** >US$500,000 and >100
  transactions). SaaS/digital products are taxable in many states; Delaware,
  Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no statewide sales tax. Sources: Wolters
  Kluwer *Economic nexus simplified* (2025-10-07); TaxCloud *Sales Tax Nexus by
  State Chart 2026* (2026-01-26); Avalara; nexusbystate.com (checked 2026-08-10).

**Data protection / privacy:**

- A UK-based controller with **no EEA establishment** that offers services to, or
  monitors, individuals in the EEA must **appoint an EEA representative in
  writing** (GDPR Article 27); the representative is a local contact point and does
  not transfer liability, and there has been at least one EU fine for failure to
  appoint. The UK has a parallel requirement that an EU representative does not
  satisfy. Sources: ICO *Receiving personal information from the EEA* (2026-01-15);
  LexisNexis practical guidance (updated 2026-05-26); BCLP (2021-06-21).
- New Zealand's **Privacy Act 2020** applies to almost every organisation
  ("agency") **regardless of size — there is no small-business exemption** — and can
  reach **overseas agencies carrying on business in New Zealand including through
  online activity**; every agency must appoint a privacy officer. Sources:
  legislation.govt.nz Privacy Act 2020 No 31 (as at 2025-11-27); Sprintlaw NZ
  (2026-07-07); compliancekit.co (2026-04-26).
- Australia's **Privacy Act 1988 small-business exemption (turnover A$3m or less)
  remains in force as at September 2026**; the exposure draft Privacy Amendment
  (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 (released 2026-08-31, submissions closed
  2026-09-18) does not remove it. Sources: OAIC *Small business*; Dentons
  (2026-09-03); Aitken (2026-09-02).

**Fundraising / charity / electronic-message rules:**

- **Ireland:** the Charities Regulator's *Guidelines for Charitable Organisations on
  Fundraising from the Public* (issued 2017-09-28 under s 14(1)(i) Charities Act
  2009) apply to fundraising through third-party agents, which require written
  contracts specifying costs and the share reaching the charitable purpose; trustees
  must be able to give a full financial account of each event. The Gambling
  Regulation Act 2024 creates a charitable/philanthropic licence type, but GRAI is
  not expected to open that licence to applications in 2026. (Auctions are sales,
  not lotteries; raffles/lotteries are the licensed activity.) Sources: Charities
  Regulator; The Wheel (2026-03-24); GRAI *Charitable/Philanthropic Licences*.
- **United States:** roughly **41 states + DC require charitable organisations to
  register before soliciting residents**, with fees commonly US$25–US$300, annual
  renewals and civil penalties (US$1,000–US$5,000 common); a **silent auction to
  raise funds is treated as solicitation** (Colorado DoS FAQ). The obligation sits
  on the organising charity, per state, including online, but a platform that
  facilitates targeted solicitation into unregistered states adds exposure for the
  organiser. Sources: IRS *Charitable solicitation – state requirements*
  (reviewed 2026-06-28); Colorado Secretary of State *Charities and Fundraisers
  FAQs*; LegalClarity (2026-05-14, 2026-08-06).
- **Australia:** the Spam Act 2003 requires consent (express or reasonably
  inferred), clear sender identification and a functional unsubscribe processed
  within 5 business days for commercial electronic messages; penalties scale per
  message/day and enforcement is rising. Source: Spam Act 2003 (Cth), Federal
  Register of Legislation; ACMA enforcement notices.

## Exact detail

- IRD: NZ$60,000 threshold, 12-month test, non-resident supplier of remote
  services, GST-registered-business carve-out; s 54B non-resident claimant
  registration for input tax recovery.
- ATO: A$75,000 threshold (A$150,000 non-profits), 10% GST, GST-registered
  recipient carve-out, simplified GST registration without an ABN.
- European Commission OSS: EUR 10,000 threshold applies only where the supplier is
  established in a single Member State; non-EU-established suppliers must use the
  Non-Union scheme or register per Member State and always charge the destination
  rate; two pieces of non-contradictory evidence for consumer location; 10-year
  records.
- Wolters Kluwer/TaxCloud: 41 states at US$100,000; AL/MS US$250,000; CA/NY/TX
  US$500,000; NY both tests; ~16 states retain a transaction-count test; IL repealed
  its transaction test effective 2026-01-01 (AK and UT repealed in 2025); DE, MT, NH
  and OR have no statewide sales tax.
- ICO: UK organisations targeting EEA individuals comply with EU GDPR and need an
  EEA representative under Article 27.
- Charities Regulator: written contracts with third-party fundraisers must specify
  costs and the percentage reaching the charitable purpose; trustees must account
  for gross raised, costs and allocation per event.
- IRS: ~41 states + DC require pre-solicitation registration; fees US$25–US$300;
  the Unified Registration Statement is accepted by 36+ jurisdictions.

## Why it is credible

Tax thresholds, privacy scope and fundraising rules come from the regulators and
legislation themselves (IRD, ATO, European Commission, ICO, NZ legislation, OAIC,
Charities Regulator, IRS). Law-firm and consultancy posts are used only where they
corroborate the primary source and are labelled accordingly.

## What it does NOT show

- It does **not** show that Aucly would cross any registration threshold: at
  current volumes (roughly seven paid auctions) it would not. The thresholds are
  relevant only if expansion succeeds.
- It does **not** quantify the cost of compliance in money or hours, and it is not
  legal or tax advice.
- It does **not** show that these rules are unusual: the UK already has VAT, UK
  GDPR and fundraising guidance, so much of this is incremental rather than novel.
- It does **not** show that the charitable-solicitation duty falls on the platform;
  it falls primarily on the organising charity.
