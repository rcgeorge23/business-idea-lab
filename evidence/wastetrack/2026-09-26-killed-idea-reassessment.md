# Evidence: WasteTrack post-kill reassessment

- **Idea / scope:** `wastetrack` — receiver-side digital waste tracking records in England
- **Register:** evidence + inference (append-only reassessment; original kill preserved)
- **Source:** GOV.UK digital waste tracking service and approved-software list
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-26
- **Credibility:** primary government service information

## Claim(s) supported

- The government service page describes digital waste tracking as a public beta opened 2026-04-28, with mandatory receiver use phased from October 2026 in England/Wales and January 2027 in Scotland/Northern Ireland. It states a £26 annual charge per receiving organisation and a spreadsheet fallback expected through at least October 2027. [S1]
- The service guidance says manual entry was reported as time-consuming and records require extensive information; it therefore encourages API-based software integration. This is evidence of usability feedback, not evidence that buyers will pay a new entrant. [S1]
- The current GOV.UK approved-software page (updated 2026-09-24) presents a broad, non-exhaustive supplier list and says a user may choose a different product if it passes 14 production approval scenarios. [S2]
- This reassessment concerns the original receiver-side idea only. The separate cross-border Article 18 seed and its LoadSnap findings are not substitutes for evidence about receiver-side unmet needs. [S3]

## Why it is credible

Both new sources are current GOV.UK service pages. The supplier list establishes market supply but does not prove that every supplier is viable or that users are satisfied. Reported service-design feedback is not independently quantified here.

## What it does NOT show

- It does not identify the number of receiver sites that lack suitable software or establish a paid exception-handling segment.
- It does not show buyer willingness to pay above the statutory charge, supplier market share, retention, margins or dissatisfaction with a named provider.
- API preference and time-consuming manual entry do not establish that the problem persists after approved integrations are available.
- The separate UK exporter/cross-border opportunity is not part of this assessment.

## Reassessment (inference)

- **Buyer:** permitted receiving-site operators are a clear regulated user/buyer population, but the separate software budget remains unproven.
- **Problem:** the mandatory digital workflow is real and manual entry is burdensome according to service feedback. The API-first approach addresses that general burden.
- **Alternatives / wedge:** the current official, non-exhaustive approved-software list confirms a large and open product ecosystem; spreadsheet fallback and the low statutory charge also constrain price. `defensible_wedge` remains `fail` for generic receiver-side tracking.
- **Distribution / economics:** integration approval, incumbent selection and site-level acquisition are material; no evidence shows a non-paid route or positive newcomer unit economics.
- **Risk / next evidence:** regulatory data/API conformance and record accuracy matter. Reopening would require a named receiver segment with a specific recurring exception not covered by listed tools/API, plus evidence of paid workaround or budget and reachable buyers. None was found in this desk reassessment.
