# Evidence: PackProof post-kill reassessment

- **Idea / scope:** `packproof` — PPWR compliance dossier, labels and declarations for small UK brands/exporters
- **Register:** evidence + inference (append-only reassessment; original kill preserved)
- **Source:** European Commission packaging/PPWR pages; PPWR Copilot pricing; UK EPR guidance
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-26
- **Credibility:** primary (EU/UK official sources) plus vendor offer

## Claim(s) supported

- Regulation (EU) 2025/40 entered into force in February 2025 and generally applies from 2026-08-12. [S1]
- The European Commission’s 2026 FAQ publication describes the harmonised EU packaging framework and covers packaging placed on the EU market, including packaging produced in or imported into the EU. Its publication page is a summary; detailed FAQ content should be checked before relying on a specific interpretation. [S2]
- PPWR Copilot currently advertises UK-priced Starter £49/month, Pro £199/month and Enterprise £499/month tiers, with dossier volumes, bulk CSV and draft EU Declaration of Conformity features; it also offers a seven-day trial and a £545 manual review. This demonstrates an advertised competing offer, not adoption. [S3]
- UK packaging EPR is a distinct domestic regime with a free GOV.UK reporting service and separate compliance schemes/obligations; it must not be conflated with the EU PPWR product proposition. [S4]
- The Commission says its Environmental Omnibus proposal affecting some PPWR provisions remains under consideration by the EU co-legislators; a proposal is not a change to the applicable law. [S1]

## Exact detail

- [S1] European Commission, Packaging and packaging waste: https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/packaging-waste_en — regulation status/application date and status of the proposed simplification checked 2026-09-26.
- [S2] European Commission, PPWR FAQ publication, published 2026-08-03: https://environment.ec.europa.eu/publications/faq-packaging-and-packaging-waste-regulation-ppwr_en — publication metadata and scope summary. The linked full FAQ was not reviewed question-by-question for this reassessment.
- [S3] PPWR Copilot pricing: https://ppwrcopilot.com/pricing — first-party price/features offer checked 2026-09-26; supplier claims are unverified.
- [S4] GOV.UK EPR for packaging collection, updated 2026-08-25: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/extended-producer-responsibility-for-packaging — separate UK EPR reporting and compliance-scheme routes.
- Related original evidence: `evidence/packproof/2026-09-21-ppwr-incumbent-landscape.md`; `evidence/packproof/2026-09-21-uk-epr-small-producer-recheck.md`.

## Why it is credible

Regulatory status and the separation between EU PPWR and UK EPR are described by the respective public authorities. PPWR Copilot’s page establishes an advertised feature/price position only; no independent adoption or commercial performance is established.

## What it does NOT show

- It does not show small UK exporters’ actual spend, usage of PPWR software, unmet requirements or satisfaction with providers.
- It does not establish the effect of the proposed Omnibus changes before enactment, or resolve every delegated/implementing measure.
- The free UK EPR reporting service does not satisfy or replace EU PPWR obligations; nor does its existence establish that every UK EPR job is adequately served.
- It does not identify a reachable, budgeted sub-segment for the proposed product.

## Reassessment (inference)

- **Buyer:** UK brands/importers placing packaging on the EU market are identifiable, but a dedicated-tool budget remains unproven.
- **Problem:** EU duties are current, while some implementation details continue to evolve. The UK EPR layer is separate and has its own free service/compliance market.
- **Alternatives / wedge:** a UK provider still advertises the proposed dossier/DoC/bulk workflow from £49/month, alongside the providers in the original register. `defensible_wedge` remains `fail` for the broad compliance suite.
- **Distribution / economics:** referral channels, buyer acquisition, maintenance/support cost, legal-review burden and achievable margins remain unknown.
- **Risk / next evidence:** a wrong compliance interpretation could have legal and market-access consequences; frequent rule changes increase maintenance burden. Reopening would require a specific settled obligation and segment demonstrably underserved by existing providers, evidence of payment/workaround and a credible reachable route. None was found in this desk reassessment.
