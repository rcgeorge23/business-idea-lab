## 2026-09-22 — Winchester City Council repairs and maintenance audit findings

- **Source:** Winchester City Council, Cabinet Committee Housing report CAB3557(H), "Repairs and Maintenance Procurement and Audit Update", 6 July 2026. https://democracy.winchester.gov.uk/documents/s35355/CAB3557H%20-%20HRA%20repairs%20and%20maintenance%20contract%20procurement%20update%20doc%201.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-22
- **Credibility:** primary (council committee report quoting an internal audit by the Southern Internal Audit Partnership)

### Claim(s) supported

- A council internal audit of the repairs and maintenance service found the control environment inadequate in three areas and required immediate action.
- The audit found a backlog and delay in processing and authorising invoices, and delay in submission of invoices.
- The audit found that for large work orders there was no breakdown of cost for labour, materials and quantities.
- The audit found 25 properties with unresolved works or indications of duplication.
- The audit found automatic approval for jobs over GBP 250, no evidence of inspections recorded on the system, and that the housing IT system (Orchard) does not store photos of works completed.
- The audit found notional jobs raised for inconsistent amounts and variations to the initial job not recorded on Orchard.

### Exact detail

> "11.16 In October 2025 the Southern Internal Audit Partnership completed their internal audit on the council's repairs and maintenance service. The audit was requested and completed as the council's s151 officer was concerned that there could be potential weakness and non-compliance. The audit sought to assess the effectiveness of controls in place, focusing on those designed to mitigate the risks to the achievement of the service objectives. The risk being a lack of compliance with financial controls for maintenance and reactive work including raising jobs and inspection of work prior to payment, and inflation of work, late submission of invoices and accuracy of transaction coding"

> "11.17 The main findings of this audit were; The audit concluded that there was no overall of assurance in 3 main areas, and that immediate action was required to address the fundamental gaps, weaknesses and compliance identified. The audit found that the system of governance, risk management and control was inadequate to manage risks to the achievement of the objectives in the areas that were audited. The main areas of weakness were; **Processes** 1. Lack of Process notes 2. Backlog and delay in processing and authorising invoices 3. Delay in submission of invoices **Raising work orders (jobs)** Notional jobs are raised in order to get these quickly passed to the contractor but they are raised for inconsistent amounts Variations to the initial job aren't recorded on Orchard **Authorising Jobs** 1. Automatic approval for jobs over £250 2. No evidence of inspections recorded on the system 3. Orchard doesn't store photos of works completed **Billing** 1. For large workorders there was no breakdown of cost for Labour, materials, quantities etc 2. 25 properties had unresolved works or indications of duplication"

> "11.22 Since January 2026, officers have been progressing the action plan to address the audit findings... A new full instruction guide process has been devised detailing all the steps to ensure that Cardo payments made on orchard (Housing IT System) are linked to the VAT invoice... Surveyors have been reminded to record all inspections onto the system- This will ensure that there is an audit trail on considerations made as part of the authorisation process for payments being approved."

### Why it is credible

A published council committee report quoting a named internal audit partnership, with specific findings and a dated action plan. It is a first-party document, not vendor marketing.

### What it does NOT show

It does not show that the council would buy a software product to fix the problem — the action plan is process notes, training and contract terms. It does not quantify the financial value of the invoice-authorisation backlog. It is one council, not a sector-wide sample.

## 2026-09-22 — Brighton & Hove housing repairs overpayment audit (GBP 300,000)

- **Source:** Brighton & Hove City Council, Audit & Standards Committee agenda item, "Overpayments on the housing repairs and improvement contract". https://democracy.brighton-hove.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=49681
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-22
- **Credibility:** primary (council committee minutes recording an internal audit and the contractor's response)

### Claim(s) supported

- An internal audit of contracted housing repairs identified potential overcharging of around GBP 300,000 by a responsive-repairs sub-contractor.
- The overcharging arose from the application of five Schedule of Rates codes: the sub-contractor charged for materials bought but not fitted, contrary to the contract.
- The sub-contractor was dismissed from the contract and the council and Mears Ltd worked to estimate and refund the overcharged amounts.
- Mears agreed to audit all responsive-repairs sub-contractors over the 17-month period, and paid for an additional quantity-surveying resource.

### Exact detail

> "Internal Audit estimate the potential overcharging was in the region of £300,000."

> "61.8 David Miles, CEO Mears Ltd stated that all responsive repairs sub-contractors working on housing repairs would be audited for works over the 17 month period to which the internal audit mainly applied. The issue arose around application of 5 schedule of rates codes by the repairs sub-contractor in question. Around 20% of the annual Mears contract was responsive repairs of which only a proportion is carried out by sub-contractors. Each job was worth about £2000. The 5 schedule of rates codes charged for materials bought by the sub-contractor and not for what was fitted, as stated in the contract."

> "61.9 ... the Head of Property & Investment/Head of Housing Strategy explained that Mears Ltd was paying the council for an additional quantity surveying resource."

### Why it is credible

Published council committee minutes naming the contractor and the mechanism, with the contractor's CEO present and responding. The mechanism (SOR codes charged for materials not fitted) is exactly the failure mode an invoice-to-SOR validation check would catch.

### What it does NOT show

It does not show the frequency of such overcharging across the sector, nor that the council bought software rather than adding quantity-surveying headcount (it added headcount). The audit is from 2015-2016, so it is dated evidence of the failure mode rather than current spend.

## 2026-09-22 — NLM Housing Association work-order and invoice-authorisation policy

- **Source:** NLM Housing Association, "Producing Work Orders and Authorising Invoices Policy", March 2026. https://www.nlmha.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Producing-Work-Orders-and-Authorising-Invoices-Policy.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-22
- **Credibility:** primary (a housing association's own published policy document)

### Claim(s) supported

- A housing association's written policy mandates a manual check of every contractor invoice against the original works order, subsequent amendments and estimated costs before authorisation.
- The Finance Officer logs the invoice, checks it against the works order, follows up queries with the contractor, and amends the works order to reflect variation costs.
- Completed invoices are passed to the Head of Assets for final checking and authorising.
- The Maintenance Officer maintains a repairs spreadsheet updated from weekly works-order reports.

### Exact detail

> "4.1 All invoices received should be passed initially to the designated Finance Officer who will: • log them on the Finance system; • Ensuring all invoices submitted by the contractor has a confirmation tenants signature slip attached confirming works completed. If no signature slip attached, invoice will not be approved until contractors proves work is completed. • inputting the invoice details on the maintenance system, following the computer procedure 'Inputting and Authorising Invoices'; • checking the invoice against the original works order, any subsequent amendments and the estimated costs etc. • following up any queries or concerns with the contractor, liaising with colleagues as required; • if required, amending the works order to reflect the authorisation of variation costs"

> "3.1 Each week the Maintenance officer will produce a report with summary details of the works orders issued the previous day and compile the list for the end of week and issue to the relevant contractors... 3.2 The MO will update the relevant repairs spreadsheet with the information from this report."

### Why it is credible

The provider's own policy document, published on its website, describing the mandated control in operational detail.

### What it does NOT show

It does not show how long the check takes, how often it catches errors, or whether the provider would buy software to perform it. It is one small housing association.

## 2026-09-22 — Rentari.ai "AI Invoice Check for Repairs" (direct competitor)

- **Source:** Rentari.ai, "AI Invoice Check for Repairs", live product page. https://www.rentari.ai/features/invoice-check ; pricing https://www.rentari.ai/pricing ; guide https://www.rentari.ai/guide
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-22
- **Credibility:** vendor (first-party product marketing; establishes the offering, not uptake or satisfaction)

### Claim(s) supported

- A live product performs exactly the proposed mechanism: a photo or PDF of a repair invoice is read, line items are extracted, and each line is compared against the accepted quote for that job and against what that contractor has charged before.
- It flags duplicate charges, including a callout already inside a quoted price, and rate changes.
- It is a co-pilot: "It flags. You decide." Nothing is refused, held or disputed automatically.
- It is priced per unit per month (USD 8–10/unit/month; USD 120/year up to 10 units) and is US-focused (dollars, 50-state leases, HUD/affordable-housing tooling).
- The platform also includes "AI vendor quality suite": multi-quote analysis, completion-photo verification, invoice-fraud detection, quote benchmarking against the local median.

### Exact detail

> "Invoices get read before they get paid. Most repair invoices are fine. The ones that are not are rarely obviously wrong, they are twenty dollars of callout that was already in the quote, or an hourly rate that quietly went up. Take a photo of the invoice and those lines get pointed at before the money leaves."

> "The comparison is not against some national average. It is against the quote you accepted for this job, and against what this particular contractor has charged you before."

> "Every line matched back to the quote it was supposed to come from. Rates compared with what this vendor charged you on past jobs. Duplicate charges caught, including a callout already inside a quoted price."

### Why it is credible

A live product page with a working demo, pricing and an app on both stores. It is vendor marketing, so it establishes the offering and the mechanism, not customer satisfaction or uptake.

### What it does NOT show

It does not show that Rentari has UK social-housing customers, that it validates against the NHF Schedule of Rates, or that it handles the provider-side works-order/SOR-code check. Its comparison basis is the accepted quote and the contractor's history, not the versioned rate book. It does not show whether UK housing providers would buy a UK-specific equivalent.

- Fixflo states it helps "ensure the right costs are applied to the contractor and job type using your schedule of rates" as part of works-order management.
- Ingentive markets a Dynamics 365 deployment that "Assists accounts payable teams with invoice processing, supplier payment queries and reconciliation workflows".
- Plentific (via FlowRunner) supports a flow that reads contractor invoices, compares the invoiced amount to the approved quote and the closing state of the work order, and separates mismatches for a human to sign off.

### Exact detail

> Fixflo: "Manage works orders more efficiently and ensure the right costs are applied to the contractor and job type using your schedule of rates."

> Ingentive: "Assists accounts payable teams with invoice processing, supplier payment queries and reconciliation workflows — reducing manual handling and accelerating payment cycles for contractor-heavy operations."

Fixflo applies SOR to works-order costing, not to inbound invoice validation. Ingentive's AP assistance is an integration service, not a SOR-specific product.
