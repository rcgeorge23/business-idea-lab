# Evidence: UK hospitality labour-market contraction and workforce pain (2025)

- **Idea / scope:** `shiftswap`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** BBC News (UKHospitality analysis of ONS data); Morning Advertiser (ONS); Restaurant Online (Pineapple + Sona workforce report)
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-20 (sources published 2025)
- **Credibility:** credible secondary — official ONS statistics and an industry-body analysis, reported by established outlets; I did not retrieve the underlying ONS datasets directly.

## Claim(s) supported

- UK hospitality has lost around 89,000 jobs since October 2024, per UKHospitality analysis of ONS data.
- The sector accounts for 53% of all UK job losses; 60% of operators say they are cutting staff.
- ONS job openings fell 5.8% to 718,000 between May and July 2025; accommodation & food service vacancies fell 19.3% year-on-year (Mar–May 2025 vs 2024), about 19,000 roles.
- Labour costs are now ~35% of revenue in UK hospitality.
- Team turnover fell from 75% to 67% over the past year (35,000+ employees in the Pineapple/Sona dataset).

## Exact detail

- BBC News, 2025-08-25 (https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c05ey2ypp92o): "Job losses in restaurants, bars, pubs and hotels total around 89,000 since last October, according to UKHospitality analysis of ONS data." UKHospitality represents more than 123,000 venues.
- Morning Advertiser, 2025-06-10: ONS data shows accommodation & food service vacancies down 19.3% year-on-year (Mar–May 2025 vs 2024), roughly 19,000 fewer roles.
- Restaurant Online, 2025-09-30 (restaurantonline.co.uk): report by Pineapple and workforce-management vendor Sona; UK hospitality team turnover fell 75% → 67% over the past year across 35,000+ employees; labour costs now 35% of revenue.
- BBC News: ONS job openings fell 5.8% to 718,000 between May and July 2025; "60% saying they are cutting staff numbers".

## Why it is credible

ONS is a primary statistical source and UKHospitality is the sector's main trade body; the figures are consistent across BBC and trade-press reporting. The Sona-linked turnover figure is weaker because it is a vendor-sponsored dataset.

## What it does NOT show

- It shows a contracting, cost-pressured labour market. It does **not** show that uncovered last-minute shifts are the binding pain, nor that venues will pay a new vendor rather than cut hours or rely on existing rota tools.
- No pricing or willingness-to-pay data for shift-cover products.
- Contraction cuts both ways: fewer staff and lower demand reduce both the need for and the ability to fund new software.
