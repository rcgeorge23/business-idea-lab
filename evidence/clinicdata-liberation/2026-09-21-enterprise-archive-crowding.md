# Evidence: crowding in legacy-application retirement and archiving

- **Idea / scope:** `clinicdata-liberation`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** archive360.com; avendata.com; docbyte.com; natuvion.com (DCS Retire 2026.3, 2026-07-01); snpgroup.com; infobelt.com
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-21
- **Credibility:** vendor claim

## Claim(s) supported

- Enterprise legacy-application retirement and read-only archiving with legal-hold and audit requirements is a crowded, well-capitalised category.
- Named providers: Archive360 (application and data retirement across ERP/CRM/SAP), AvenDATA ViewBox (Fortune 500 clients, IRS/SEC readiness), Docbyte Vault (healthcare example), Natuvion DCS Retire 2026.3 (2026-07-01, adds enterprise AI), Infobelt, SNP Group Kyano Datafridge.
- SNP's published case: TotalEnergies retired 17 SAP ECC systems using the product.

## Exact detail

- These vendors sell to enterprises on retention/audit grounds, not to small clinics on continuity grounds.
- The Cherwell-specific archive vendor (Synapse Software, separate entry) is a narrower niche player, which shows the pattern repeats at smaller scale.

## Why it is credible

Multiple independent vendors with named reference customers occupy the enterprise tier; the crowding is directly observable from their own sites.

## What it does NOT show

- It does not show that the enterprise archiving incumbents address small UK clinical practices; they largely do not.
- It does not show that the small-clinic tier is therefore unserved — ValueStreamAI and receiving-vendor migrations already operate there.
- It does not show margins or switching behaviour in the small-clinic tier.
