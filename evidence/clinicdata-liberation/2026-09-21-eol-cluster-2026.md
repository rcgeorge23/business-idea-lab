# Evidence: 2026 end-of-life cluster in clinical and practice software

- **Idea / scope:** `clinicdata-liberation`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** nookal.com (BP Allied switch-off), 2026-06-22; synapsesoftware.com (Cherwell EOL), 2026-02-17; provet.com Milestone Vet case study (RoboVet on-prem EOL), 2026-08-10
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-21
- **Credibility:** credible secondary / vendor claim

## Claim(s) supported

- BP Allied (allied-health practice software) switches off on 31 July 2026: no updates, fixes or support afterwards; a competitor markets a guided migration (export → load → sanity-check → switch) including clinical notes and history.
- Cherwell Service Management is end-of-life on 31 December 2026 with cloud instances shut down; organisations with HIPAA (6+ years) and SOX (7 years) retention obligations must either migrate all history (tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars), export flat files (losing relationships/form views/attachments), keep unsupported software, or buy a purpose-built read-only archive.
- A UK veterinary practice migrated off on-premise, end-of-life RoboVet to a cloud PIMS in June 2026, illustrating that forced migrations are happening in the UK clinical long tail.

## Exact detail

- BP Allied: no published export path described; migration help is offered by the receiving vendor.
- Cherwell: retention obligations sit with the customer, not the vendor; a third-party archive reading the SQL Server backup is the documented alternative.
- These are dated, fixed switch-offs, not rumoured deprecations.

## Why it is credible

The switch-off dates are published by the vendors/communities affected and the consequences are described by parties with no incentive to overstate the difficulty of staying (the archive vendor) and by parties selling the migration (receiving vendor) — both point the same way on the forced nature of the event.

## What it does NOT show

- It does not show how many UK niche clinical platforms are in the same position, nor the size of the addressable population.
- Vendor pages do not prove that clinics will pay a third party rather than accept data loss or a receiving-vendor migration.
- It does not establish a legal duty to preserve clinical records for the UK private/specialist tier.
