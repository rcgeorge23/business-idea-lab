# Evidence: Oddbox financial trajectory (FY2022–FY2025)

- **Idea / scope:** `wonkybox`
- **Register:** evidence
- **Source:** The Grocer (four articles, 2023–2026); The Times (2025-11)
- **Accessed / dated:** 2026-09-20
- **Credibility:** credible secondary — trade and national press reporting filed accounts. I did not retrieve the Companies House filings directly, so figures are as reported.

## Claim(s) supported

- Oddbox, the leading UK surplus-produce box, has been loss-making for several consecutive years despite growing then stabilising revenue around £27–32m.
- Revenue has fallen and losses persisted as the company cut marketing and staff.
- Operating losses widened 98.7% in the year to 30 June 2025.
- The company has publicly described significant subscription churn.

## Exact detail

- The Grocer, 2023-04-05 (article id 678042): FY to 30 Jun 2022 pre-tax **loss £4.8m** (prior year +£540k profit) despite revenue **+8.4% to £32.3m**; ~£30m raised in total; valued >£100m.
- The Grocer, 2024-09-21 (article id 689524): FY2023 revenue **−8% to £29.7m**; pre-tax loss **£3.7m**; EBITDA loss **−£2.9m**.
- The Grocer, 2025-01-21 (article id 700063): FY2024 revenue **−7.7% to £27.4m**; pre-tax loss **£1.7m**; "It stopped marketing to acquire new customers and laid off more than 40 staff".
- The Grocer, 2026-03-12 (article id 716386): year to 30 Jun 2025 revenue **+£300k to £27.7m**, but operating losses **widened 98.7% to £1.4m**; prices frozen to retain customers.
- The Times, 2025-11-24/25: Oddbox ~£27m turnover; from Feb 2025 shifted from grower-centric to customer-centric because "we had a lot of people stop our box [subscription], a lot of churn".

Full article URLs were not captured verbatim during this run (partial slugs/article IDs only, as above); figures should be re-verified against Companies House before any reopening.

## Why it is credible

The Grocer is the established UK grocery trade title and The Times is a national newspaper, both reporting filed accounts. The trajectory is internally consistent across four years.

## What it does NOT show

- Does not prove no entrant can succeed — only that the category leader, with scale and £30m of capital, has not achieved profitable unit economics.
- Does not isolate delivery, spoilage, CAC or churn as the dominant cause.
