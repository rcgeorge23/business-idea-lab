## One-sentence proposition

> Food-waste-conscious households want affordable odd-shaped produce; we provide a local weekly surplus-produce box at ~£12, delivered or collected.

## Buyer

- Who exactly pays: consumers buying a weekly grocery box.
- Who uses it: the household.
- Evidence: `evidence/wonkybox/2026-09-20-incumbent-box-schemes.md`

## Problem

- What is painful/frequent/expensive: food waste is a live consumer concern and grocery is a frequent purchase, but for the buyer a surplus box is a preference, not a need.
- Current alternatives and their weaknesses: Oddbox (founded 2016, ~£27m revenue, ~85 staff) and Wonky Veg Boxes (from £10.49) already serve it; supermarkets sell cheap imperfect produce.
- Evidence: `evidence/wonkybox/2026-09-20-oddbox-financials.md`

## Mechanism / wedge

- What we would actually do: source surplus/odd produce locally and run a weekly subscription box.
- Why it is defensible against the cheapest credible incumbent: it is not — the category leader has scale and £30m of capital and still loses money, and a "local" variant is not defensible against a national brand or a £1 supermarket line.
- Evidence: `evidence/wonkybox/2026-09-20-oddbox-financials.md`

## Distribution

- Route to the first 10 buyers without paid acquisition: none credible; Oddbox cut marketing to new customers and revenue then fell, showing how dependent the model is on paid acquisition.
- Evidence: `evidence/wonkybox/2026-09-20-oddbox-financials.md`

## Economics (assumptions labelled)

- Price hypothesis: £10–£18 per box plus delivery (matches observed incumbent pricing).
- Cost drivers: produce sourcing, cold chain/delivery, spoilage, packaging, customer acquisition.
- Contribution margin at realistic scale: implausible — Oddbox was loss-making at £27–32m revenue, with operating losses widening 98.7% in FY2025 despite price freezes.

- Reach, skills, motivation: `null` — no evidence recorded.

## Adversarial case (strongest case this is wrong)

The category leader has raised ~£30m, reached ~£27m revenue, and still cannot make money; its losses widened most recently. That is close to a natural experiment proving the unit economics of DTC surplus grocery are hostile: perishable inventory, delivery cost, spoilage, and high churn (Oddbox itself cited "a lot of churn"). A tiny entrant faces the same physics with none of the scale, and would be competing against supermarkets that can sell imperfect produce below cost as a traffic driver. "Local" lowers delivery density, which makes the economics worse, not better.

## Strongest supporting case

Consumer interest in food waste is genuine and the incumbents' price points (£10–£18) are affordable. A pickup-only, B2B or community-hub model avoids delivery cost and could serve a niche the national players ignore.
