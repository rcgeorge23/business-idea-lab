## One-sentence proposition

> A guided PPWR compliance tool for small UK packaged-goods brands and EU importers — packaging dossier, QR labelling and EU Declaration of Conformity generation — at a low monthly price.

## Why now?

- **What changed:** Regulation (EU) 2025/40 (PPWR) generally applies from 12 August 2026, adding PFAS limits in food-contact packaging, traceability/labelling duties, technical documentation, an EU Declaration of Conformity (importer retention), and per-Member-State EPR registration. UK exporters selling packaged goods into the EU are in scope.
- **Changed date:** 2026-08-12
- **Evidence:** `evidence/packproof/2026-09-21-ppwr-incumbent-landscape.md`
- **Why it materially improves the opportunity:** a hard, dated, EU-wide compliance duty for a large population of brand owners and importers did not exist in this form three years ago.
- **Did competitors respond?** Yes — PPWR Copilot (UK, from £49/mo), PPWRify (Germany), PPWR Connect, and Trace One (marketing to "9,000+ brands") already sell PPWR workflows. The market formed ahead of the deadline.
- **Strength:** strong for the regulatory change; adverse for a new entrant because incumbency is already established.

## Buyer

Small UK packaged-goods brands and importers selling into the EU, and their agents. Buyer is plausible; budget for this specific tool is not evidenced.

## Problem

New PPWR labelling, DoC and EPR duties with per-country variation are confusing and time-bound, and non-compliance blocks EU market access. Severity is credible (regulatory), frequency is low (periodic compliance), and existing budget was not evidenced.

## Mechanism / wedge

Guided packaging dossier plus QR label and DoC generator. Directly overlaps multiple live vendors; differentiation capped at 2 for direct incumbent overlap.

## Distribution

No non-paid route evidenced. Compliance-scheme and accountant referrals are plausible only; distribution capped at 2.

## Economics (assumptions labelled)

- **Assumption:** £15–£30/mo would be sustainable.
- **Evidence against:** a UK incumbent already advertises £49/mo for 10 dossiers, anchoring price and implying a heavier build than a micro-tool.
- Modelled only; economics scored 2 with low confidence.

## Founder fit

Would require packaging/regulatory domain knowledge and ongoing delegated-act monitoring. Not evidenced for our owner.

## Adversarial case (strongest case this is wrong)

This is a compliance-software category with several funded, product-complete competitors, one of which is UK-branded and cheap. Regulatory deadlines concentrate demand but also concentrate competition; a late micro-entrant competes on price against better-capitalised, more complete tools, while the EU still has ~30 implementing acts pending that change requirements and reward incumbents' monitoring capacity. The horizontal "small UK brand" buyer is fragmented and expensive to reach.

## Strongest supporting case

The pending delegated acts and the separate UK EPR regime leave gaps incumbents may underserve (see seed). A narrow wedge inside one settled duty could be viable later — but not this broad tool.
