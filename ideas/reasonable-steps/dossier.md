## One-sentence proposition

> A low-cost tool that helps UK SMEs with 10–100 staff evidence the steps they take to prevent harassment and third-party harassment, ready for the 30 October 2026 "all reasonable steps" duty.

## Why now?

- **What changed:** The Employment Rights Act 2025 (c.36) raises the preventive duty from "reasonable steps" to "all reasonable steps" (amending Equality Act 2010 s.40A) and adds a duty not to permit third-party harassment, with liability unless all reasonable steps were taken. The commencement date is 30 October 2026; the statute itself contemplates recordable steps (risk assessments, incident monitoring/recording, policy, training, complaint handling) that regulations may specify.
- **Changed date:** 2026-10-30
- **Evidence:** `evidence/reasonable-steps/2026-09-21-harassment-duty-and-incumbents.md`
- **Why it materially improves the opportunity:** the standard of proof and the scope of duty both tighten on a fixed date, creating a new evidence/record-keeping job for employers that did not exist in this form before.
- **Did competitors respond?** Partially — free Acas/EHRC guidance and paid generic training exist, and HR platforms cover adjacent policy/record needs; no dedicated low-cost SME evidence tool was surfaced by these searches. That absence is a gap in the searched sources, not proof of an open market.
- **Strength:** strong for the legal change; the *commercial* opportunity is unproven.

## Buyer

UK SME employers with roughly 10–100 staff, no dedicated HR system, and an owner or office manager who handles HR alongside other duties. Buyer and willingness to pay are assumptions (budget inferred), so `buyer_budget_clarity` is capped at 2 and overall confidence is low.

## Problem

From 30 October 2026 an employer must be able to show it took all reasonable steps and did not permit third-party harassment. Most small employers have no single system of record for risk assessment, incidents, policy acknowledgement and training. Frequency is low (a periodic legal event), severity is material (tribunal exposure and uncapped compensation in related areas), and existing budget is not evidenced.

## Mechanism / wedge

A low-cost (£12–£20/mo) "reasonable steps" evidence toolkit: guided risk assessment, incident log, policy templates with acknowledgement records, training tracker and an exportable evidence pack. The wedge is the specific evidence/audit job plus the fixed 30 October 2026 trigger — not generic HR content, which is free.

## Distribution

Direct outreach to SMEs (owner/office manager), accountant/bookkeeper and employment-law referrals, and business networks. Non-paid channels are plausible but unproven; distribution capped at 2 (plausible only). The buyer is fragmented, which raises acquisition cost.

## Adversarial case (strongest case this is wrong)

The authoritative content is free from Acas and the EHRC, and the government has said employer guidance will be published in advance. Many SMEs already pay for an HR platform or an employment-law adviser who will add this duty as part of existing cover. "All reasonable steps" is explicitly proportionate to resources, which weakens urgency for the smallest firms. The tool may be a feature, not a product, and the fixed deadline may be too small a trigger for a durable subscription.

## Strongest supporting case

The duty is law from a specific date, it explicitly requires *evidence* of steps rather than mere intent, third-party harassment broadens exposure to customers/contractors, and no dedicated cheap tool was found. If owners feel personally exposed and lack a system of record, a small subscription that produces a defensible evidence pack is a plausible purchase — but only if they will not simply adopt free templates.

## Cheapest decisive experiment

- **Assumption under test:** UK SME employers (10–100 staff, no dedicated HR system) will pay >=£12/mo for a "reasonable steps" evidence tool rather than rely on free guidance or existing HR software.
- **Proposed test:** non-network interviews with 10 target employers, showing a paper prototype and seeking a concrete commitment (deposit or signed letter of intent at >=£12/mo), plus one non-network recruitment channel.
- **Pre-fixed decision rule:** proceed iff >=3 of 10 commit at >=£12/mo AND >=1 non-network channel yields >=5 conversations; iterate if 1–2 commitments or price settles at <=£8; stop if 0 commitments or the dominant reaction is that free guidance/HR software suffices.
- **Cost bound:** £20 money, 10 human-hours, 7 calendar days.
