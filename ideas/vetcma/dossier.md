## One-sentence proposition

> Every UK companion-animal practice will be legally required to publish a
> standardised price list and adopt estimate/bill/complaint duties on a staged
> 2026-2027 timetable, so a low-cost compliance tool for the ~2,382 independent
> practices could be sold - except that at least four visible providers, two of
> them free forever, already occupy exactly that job.

## Why now?

- **What changed:** The CMA concluded its veterinary market investigation and
  published its final decision report; it then published the draft *Veterinary
  Services Market Investigation Order 2026* for consultation. The Order is due
  by statute and the first transparency duties bite later in 2026.
- **Changed date:** 2026-03-24 (final report); 2026-07-21 (draft Order);
  statutory deadline 2026-09-23.
- **Evidence:** `evidence/vetcma/2026-09-21-cma-order-and-incumbent-landscape.md`
  (CMA case page; BVA; draft Order PDF; Cloud 9 Vets/GlobeNewswire; Funding Order
  2026; provider pages).
- **Why it materially improves the opportunity:** the duty is mandatory,
  industry-wide, dated and reinforced by a funded RCVS monitoring platform; a
  previously voluntary transparency task becomes unavoidable.
- **Did competitors respond?** Yes, heavily. VetGuard (free price-list tool,
  £49/mo Complete), pricebook.vet (free forever base tool), vetcompliance.co.uk
  (free scanner), Vetstoria (CMA-compliant websites/booking) and PMS incumbent
  Provet (says its itemised invoicing and estimates already cover the duties).
- **Strength:** strong.

**Outcome:** Fails the novelty check. The only reason to continue would be an
evidenced, under-served sub-job; none was found large enough to carry a
business. Recorded as a seed (`vet-estimate-bridge`) rather than pursued.

## Buyer

Owner/manager of an independent UK companion-animal practice. VetGuard's public
aggregation of 4,810 practices reports 2,382 independent (50%), 1,889 big-6
corporate (39%), 473 smaller groups (10%), 66 charity (1%). The corporate
groups are served by their PMS/website vendors; independents are the nominal
opportunity but are exactly the segment the free tools target.

## Problem

From late 2026-2027, practices must publish a 36-service price list in a
prescribed format, give written estimates for pathways likely to cost £500+,
issue itemised bills, disclose ownership, and run a complaints process.
However, the problem is a bounded compliance task with abundant free tooling,
not a painful, repeated, under-served job.

## Mechanism / wedge

No defensible wedge identified. The candidate mechanism (hosted price list +
website widget + estimate/bill templates + complaints record + RCVS filing) is
already shipped by multiple providers at £0-£49/month.

## Distribution

The natural unpaid routes (RCVS, BVA, FIVP, regional practice-manager networks)
are also the routes the incumbents use, and free tools travel them faster.

## Economics (assumptions labelled)

- *Assumption:* independents would pay £19-£49/month for the recurring
  attestation/complaints record.
- *Evidence:* VetGuard prices Complete at £49/month and anchor it against the
  regulator's expected levy; pricebook.vet gives the price-list job away free.
- *Inference:* price competition has already collapsed the price-list job to
  zero and the recurring job to a thin subscription with no evidence of
  conversion. Margins unproven.

## Founder fit

Not assessed in depth (killed at novelty/filter stage).

## Adversarial case (strongest case this is wrong)

The strongest case for the idea is that the recurring obligations - a signed
yearly compliance attestation, a dated complaints record, RCVS re-filing on
every price change - are real recurring work, and VetGuard charges £49/month for
them. But that is precisely the incumbent's position, already taken, with free
tools as the acquisition funnel. Competing head-on from a standing start is
value-destructive; the plausible adjacent job (estimate-to-bill bridge) is too
small to be a business on its own.

## Strongest supporting case

A real, dated, mandatory, industry-wide change with a clearly identified buyer
cohort and a funded regulator platform - a textbook "why now?". It fails on
supply-side crowding, not on demand.

## Cheapest decisive experiment

None proposed. The candidate is killed on `defensible_wedge` (commodity
positioning with free alternatives), so a disconfirming test is moot. If
revisited, the cheapest decisive test is a landing page offered alongside a
free price-list tool, measured against the free alternatives.
