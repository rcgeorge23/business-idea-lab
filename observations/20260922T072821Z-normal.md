dissatisfaction, market failure or change without a product proposition. Rows are
not scored, carry no evidence level, and confer no inherited positive evidence on

## Triage false-negative audit

| Field | Record |

| Observation selected | O1 — MIS migration data cleansing and Data Counts labour |
| Why selected (vs other rejections) | It is the strongest practitioner/problem evidence in the pool: the incumbent's own process documentation describes a 12-week, multi-phase manual labour process with a documented failure mode (court orders do not migrate), and it is the observation closest to a latent-opportunity hypothesis. It was rejected because the incoming MIS and an established consultancy market occupy the seam — exactly the "competitor existence vs adequate occupation" confusion the audit exists to test. |
| Original triage reasoning | "Demonstrated occupation: the migration workflow is owned end-to-end by the incoming MIS and by an established paid consultancy market; no accessible unserved seam for a new entrant." |
| Additional evidence checked | Arbor's own onboarding guides (2026-02 to 2026-07) confirm the incoming MIS runs the migration and supplies the Data Cleansing and Counts Pack; Involve Education's migration service page confirms a paid reconciliation-pack service with record counts, financial totals, attendance percentages and spot-check samples; School ICT, School Business Services and MIS Data Solutions all sell migration/data-cleansing consultancy. |
| Confusion tests | Existence vs satisfaction: the alternatives demonstrably satisfy the job (the incumbent supplies the counts; consultancies supply reconciliation packs) — not mere existence. Feature vs solution: the reconciliation pack is a complete service, not a feature. Enterprise vs niche: the service is sold to individual schools, not only large trusts. Claims vs capability: the consultancy pages describe delivered services with named artefacts. One-shot vs recurring: the migration is one-shot, so even a genuine gap has unattractive recurring economics. |
| Outcome | **Upheld.** |
| Implication for triage depth | Triage depth was adequate on this observation: the rejection rests on demonstrated occupation and one-shot economics, not on competitor existence alone. No change to triage depth indicated. |

  practitioner = forums, communities, support threads, job ads, service pricing;

  this pool rephrases an Aucly feature. O17 is the prior education-adjacent
  rejection O16 from `observations/20260921T100247Z-normal.md`, re-recorded with
  its original rejection preserved. No seed mentions school/education/pupil/

  observations. Both distinguish engagement from demonstrable learning benefit:
  the DfE market assessment and the parliamentary evidence both note that pupil
  impact is not established for most tools, and the evidence bar (not the tooling)
  is the binding constraint. Safety, privacy, accessibility and teacher-oversight
  risks are material for any pupil-facing product and would require specialist
  review; no pupil-facing candidate was promoted.
- **Boundaries:** no source was accessed through authentication; no school, staff
  member, parent or child was contacted; no pupil data was collected; no pilot,
  spend or public claim was made.
