## Seed re-check (fresh, non-inheriting)

Seed `vet-estimate-bridge` was researched from scratch; no `vetcma` score, evidence level or favourable assumption was inherited. The CMA’s final 2026 Order is now published and confirms the trigger and phased deadlines. That corrects the seed’s earlier draft/order timing, but does not validate software demand. Provet directly advertises the core written-estimate-to-invoice/payment flow; ezyVet occupies adjacent charge capture; Vetstoria covers booking/web transparency. These vendor claims do not prove adoption or universal coverage, but the sweep found no direct practice-side workaround, system-migration gap, buyer budget or reachable distribution route. **Disposition: drop the seed as framed**, without creating or killing an idea. This is not a claim that all CMA-related veterinary software is unnecessary.

## Exactly-one triage false-negative audit

**Selected rejected observation: O12 (Haringey invoice capture exceptions).** It was selected because a specific £79,200 award is stronger evidence of money already moving than most rows and could be incorrectly dismissed as merely a public-sector service. Original reasoning: the notice purchases scanning/validation services into SAP, with no software license, evidence of dissatisfied staff, or separate product buyer. Re-check: read the award scope, term/option and below-threshold/no-competition basis; compared the separately inspected Homerton integrated-finance award (O13–O15), which buys a broader managed service.

**Confusion tests:** existence vs satisfaction—outsourced work is evidenced, dissatisfaction is not; feature vs solution—a managed service is not evidence a standalone software tool solves the exceptions; enterprise vs niche—large SAP deployments do not establish small/local-authority reachability; claims vs capability—the award documents scope, not measured outcomes; one-shot vs recurring—Haringey’s short initial term/option and Homerton’s longer managed service do not show recurring standalone-product demand. **Outcome: upheld.** Triage should retain public awards as money-moving signals but require a distinct unmet software job, buyer and route before promotion. No rejection rested on competitor existence alone.
