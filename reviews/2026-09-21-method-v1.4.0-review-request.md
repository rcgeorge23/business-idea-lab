## What changed and why

One change: a **source-class budget** in `method/discovery.md`, applied at run
time by `method/run-protocol.md`.

> At least 2 of the maximum 3 candidate slots must originate from non-regulatory
> source classes. At most 1 candidate may primarily originate from
> legislation/regulation. At least one previously underexplored non-regulatory
> source class must actually be searched.

Motivation is empirical, not theoretical. Four consecutive discovery runs
produced a `defensible_wedge` kill cluster (agentready, packproof, vetcma,
prsregister, propident, wastetrack, bikpayroll) because easily searchable
regulatory/mandate discontinuities consumed the discovery budget by default.
Both the issue #4 convergence review and the independent v1.3.0 review agreed
the filter is working correctly and the sourcing should change. The budget is a
**discovery constraint only**: it confers no scoring advantage, and fewer than
three candidates, an all-kill run or zero advances remain valid outcomes.

Recording requirements were extended so a run summary must show the classes
actually searched (regulatory vs non-regulatory, successful **and**
unsuccessful), candidate provenance, the class each candidate qualifies under
and why, and whether the budget was satisfied. The no-filler rule is explicit:
candidates are never manufactured to satisfy the quota.

Unchanged: scorecard weights, threshold 65, hard-filter semantics
(`pass`/`unknown`/`fail`), `defensible_wedge`, evidence-level definitions,
lifecycle gates, candidate maximum 3, advancement maximum 1, falsification-first
behaviour.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Does a sourcing budget risk quota-driven or performative research despite the
   explicit no-filler rule? What would make that observable in the artefacts?
2. Is a sourcing constraint the right response to a `defensible_wedge` cluster,
   or is it treating a symptom? What would falsify the sourcing diagnosis?
3. Does the budget preserve falsification-first behaviour, or could it bias
   candidate *framing* (e.g. relabelling a mandate-derived product as an
   integration play) while leaving the underlying problem the same?
4. Under what evidence should the budget be retained, changed or removed for
   subsequent runs? Is one post-change run enough to judge it?
