## Findings

The source-class budget is a justified discovery-only experiment and should not be treated as a scoring advantage. The post-change run provides useful empirical evidence: all three candidates were non-regulatory, previously underexplored classes were genuinely searched, failed searches were recorded, and no filler candidate was required to satisfy the quota. This answers the principal quota-risk concern favourably.

The run also falsifies the stronger hypothesis that regulatory concentration was the main cause of the repeated `defensible_wedge` failures. Diversifying source classes changed the subject matter of the candidates but not the rejection mechanism. That is evidence that the bottleneck is now broader hypothesis-space coverage and framing rather than simply regulatory sourcing.

The budget should therefore be retained for now because it successfully prevents the prior regulatory default, but it should not be tightened or interpreted as a route to higher scores.

## Framing risk

The main risk is relabelling essentially the same opportunity shape as a non-regulatory integration or workflow play. This should be observable through provenance and the underlying causal trigger, not the candidate's chosen label. The issue #6 artefacts do not show material quota gaming: the candidates genuinely arose from platform pricing/access, EOL/migration and agentic-commerce changes.

## What would falsify continued use of the budget

Reconsider or remove the budget if several broader-funnel runs show that:

- regulatory observations consistently produce materially stronger candidates than non-regulatory observations and the quota excludes them;
- the quota causes weak/filler candidates or performative source searches;
- provenance becomes ambiguous enough that workers can satisfy the rule by relabelling the same underlying opportunity; or
- a broader observation-stage funnel makes candidate-slot quotas unnecessary because source diversity is achieved before promotion.

One post-change run is enough to conclude that v1.4 operated as designed, but not enough to conclude that the 2-of-3 allocation is globally optimal.

## Recommendation for the next method experiment

The evidence supports changing discovery breadth rather than evaluation. A broad lightweight observation pool followed by shallow triage and at most three full candidates is a reasonable next experiment. It should explicitly admit both change-driven opportunities and persistent market failures such as poor/expensive niche software, manual re-keying and awkward incumbent integrations.

Do not weaken `defensible_wedge`, the 65 threshold, hard-filter semantics, evidence levels or lifecycle gates as part of that experiment.

## Condition that would change the verdict

Request changes if future artefacts show quota-driven filler, provenance relabelling, or exclusion of stronger opportunities solely to satisfy source allocation. Otherwise v1.4 is an acceptable sourcing guardrail while broader discovery is tested.
