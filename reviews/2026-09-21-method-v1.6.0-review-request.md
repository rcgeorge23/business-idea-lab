## What changed and why

### 1. The missing-why-now cap is archetype-aware

`method/discovery.md`, `method/scorecard.md` and `method/lifecycle.md`. The v1.2.0
cap (absent/unevidenced why-now -> `differentiation <= 2` and
`problem_severity_frequency <= 2` at `Plausible`/`Promising`, lifted at `Demand
evidence`) now distinguishes the two discovery archetypes:

- **Change-driven candidates:** the v1.2.0 rule applies unchanged.
- **Persistent-market-failure candidates:** no discontinuity is required or invented.
  The cap lifts only when an **evidence-backed persistence thesis** answers "why has
  this problem remained inadequately solved despite existing alternatives?" and
  affirmatively supports **both**:
  1. continued buyer pain / cost / workaround despite alternatives that exist and are
     reachable for the target segment (e.g. repeated recent practitioner complaints
     about the same narrow workflow; demonstrated manual workarounds despite incumbent
     availability; incumbent pricing/minimum-contract economics systematically
     excluding the segment; credible switching/data-access barriers combined with
     continuing pain; products that technically ship a feature the segment cannot
     practically or economically use); and
  2. a credible, present-tense persistence mechanism (incumbent architecture/channel
     constraints; vendor economics; buyer fragmentation; a recent fall in the cost of
     building the missing piece).

  A weak, absent or speculative thesis leaves the cap in place. The thesis is **not a
  scoring bonus**: it never lifts any other dimension cap, never upgrades a
  `defensible_wedge` or other hard filter, and unsupported narratives ("incumbents
  don't care", "the market is too small") are explicitly insufficient.

### 2. Competitor existence is not wedge failure

`method/discovery.md` (shallow triage) and `method/run-protocol.md` (step 3.2). Triage
rejections for crowding now require evidence that credible alternatives
**demonstrably and adequately occupy the exact proposed seam for the defined buyer** -
not merely that competitors exist, are advertised, ship an adjacent feature, or serve
a different segment. The `defensible_wedge` filter itself is unchanged and still fails
where incumbents genuinely occupy the seam (the Aucly calibration case is the
standing reminder that a viable niche need not have zero competitors).

### 3. Sampled triage false-negative audit

`method/discovery.md` (new subsection), `method/run-protocol.md` (new step 3.4),
templates and prompts. Every normal funnel run now independently re-checks **exactly
one** triage-rejected observation, selected from the most promising/high-ambiguity
rejections (relatively strong practitioner/problem evidence rejected because an
incumbent or free alternative appeared to occupy the seam). The check is cheap and
bounded to roughly the cost of one observation, and tests five specific confusions:

1. competitor existence vs adequate customer satisfaction;
2. a feature vs a complete solution;
3. enterprise availability vs accessibility to the target niche;
4. vendor claims vs demonstrated capability;
5. one-shot migration/service availability vs attractive recurring product economics.

The record (observation selected, why, original reasoning, additional evidence
checked, upheld/overturned, implication for triage depth) goes in the run summary and
the pool file. A single overturn does not change the method; repeated overturns are a
trigger to review triage depth. The funnel and its 15-20 pool size are retained
provisionally; this version does not resize them.

## Regression evidence

- **Aucly pre-launch** (`ideas/aucly/pre-launch-assessment.md`, simulated under
  1.2.0, 49.5, parked at `desk-screened`): the persistence thesis can now be recorded
  without manufacturing a discontinuity, but the limited pre-launch evidence still
  leaves the score and state unchanged. The rule was not tuned to make Aucly pass, and
  the historical assessment file is not rescored.
- **`shiftswap` (44.2), `wonkybox` (36.8), `grantscout` (55.8)**: all remain killed
  for their recorded hard-filter failures/vetoes (non-paid distribution, defensible
  wedge, network effects, margins, all-optimistic viability). Persistent scheduling,
  food-waste and grant-hunting pain does not rescue them because their failures are
  not missing-why-now caps.
- **First sampled triage audit (O14, insurance broker submission re-keying)**: the
  v1.5 rejection was cheaply re-checked and **upheld** on demonstrated incumbent
  occupation (live submission-automation vendors with measured deployments plus AMS
  connectivity), not on competitor existence alone. The record is appended to
  `observations/20260921T091851Z-normal.md` and summarised in
  `retrospectives/2026-09-21-issue8-archetype-cap-regression.md`.

Unchanged: scorecard weights, threshold 65, hard-filter semantics
(`pass`/`unknown`/`fail`), `defensible_wedge`, evidence-level definitions, lifecycle
gates, candidate maximum 3, advancement maximum 1, pool size 15-20, falsification-first
behaviour. No discovery cycle was run for this version.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Is the two-part persistence test tight enough to stop unsupported "evergreen pain"
   narratives from bypassing the cap, while still being usable for a genuine
   archetype-B candidate whose evidence is practitioner complaints plus a structural
   mechanism?
2. Is "exactly one" sampled triage audit per funnel run the right cadence and cost, or
   should sampling be weighted by pool size or by how many rejections cited crowding?
3. Does the competitor-existence clarification risk weakening `defensible_wedge` in
   practice by inviting argument at the margin, given the filter text itself is
   unchanged?
4. Are the three regression outcomes and the O14 audit record sufficient evidence that
   this version corrects the 1.5.0 defects without reviving generic or crowded
   propositions?
