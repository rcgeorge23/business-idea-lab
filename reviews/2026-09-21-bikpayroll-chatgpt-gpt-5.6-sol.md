## Findings

The kill follows the precommitted experiment rule on the repository evidence. The scan identifies at least two concrete adjacent/platform capabilities occupying the proposed provider-to-payroll seam: The Electric Car Scheme claims direct API/SFTP monthly payroll inputs, and Zhoosh describes effective-dated benefit changes producing per-period employee taxable values for payroll. Zest/Zellis and IRIS provide additional evidence that the surrounding category is absorbing the workflow.

The evidence is vendor-published and does not establish market share or large-scale adoption. That uncertainty would matter if the decision rule required proof of incumbent dominance; it did not. The experiment was deliberately designed to falsify the claim that the seam was unserved. On that narrower question, the threshold is met.

The counter-evidence — manual entry in Sage/BrightPay and uneven insurer reconciliation — demonstrates remaining friction but does not establish a defensible standalone bridge. A small-employer/no-benefits-platform segment should not be promoted automatically as a seed unless separate evidence identifies a materially different buyer/job and a reason incumbent/provider solutions do not reach it.

The repeated `defensible_wedge` cluster is better interpreted as a discovery-sourcing problem than as evidence that the filter is too strict.

## Reopening condition

Reopen only if new evidence shows the cited capabilities are materially unavailable in practice to the target segment, or that a clearly defined segment has recurring provider-to-payroll reconciliation pain that adjacent platforms cannot economically serve.
