## Overall assessment

The opportunity-observation funnel is a useful change and should be **retained**. The first empirical run shows that it materially broadened the search space at low cost: 20 observations across 12 source classes were reduced to two full candidates, without filler, and obvious incumbent-captured opportunities were rejected before expensive candidate research.

The requested change is narrow. The run exposed two methodological issues that should be corrected before treating v1.5 as settled:

1. the existing `why_now` score cap can systematically penalise genuine persistent-market-failure candidates merely because no recent discontinuity exists; and
2. triage-only rejections currently have no sampled false-negative audit, even though the funnel now makes 90% of discovery decisions at that stage.

Neither finding justifies weakening `defensible_wedge`, lowering the threshold, or changing evidence standards.

## 1. Pool size: retain 15–20 for now

The first run does not show quantity-over-quality failure.

The pool contained 20 observations, 12 source classes and 17 problem areas. The repository records explicit incumbent/free-alternative checks and triage reasons, and only two observations were promoted. There is no evidence that the worker padded the pool to manufacture candidates.

Do **not** increase the pool yet. Twenty observations consumed roughly 38/40 web lookups; a larger pool would likely reduce evidence quality per observation before we know whether breadth is the binding constraint.

Treat 15–20 as a provisional operating range rather than an optimised constant. Reassess after several runs.

## 2. Add a sampled false-negative audit for triage rejections

The funnel changes where rejection happens. In the first run, 18/20 observations — 90% — were rejected before becoming ideas. Those rejections do not receive the killed-idea false-negative process.

That creates a new blind spot: a cheap shallow incumbent search may reject an observation because products exist without establishing that those products actually solve the narrow buyer/job well enough.

Do not deeply review all triage rejects; that would defeat the funnel. Instead introduce a small audit sample.

Recommended rule:

> After each normal funnel run, independently re-check **one triage-rejected observation**, selected from the most promising/highest-ambiguity rejected observations rather than purely at random. Prefer cases rejected for `crowded/incumbent-served` where practitioner pain evidence was relatively strong.

The audit should ask whether shallow triage confused:
- existence of competitors with adequate satisfaction;
- a feature with a complete solution;
- enterprise availability with accessibility to the target niche;
- vendor claims with demonstrated capability;
- one-shot migration/service supply with an unattractive recurring product.

If the audit repeatedly overturns triage decisions, increase triage depth or sampling. If it rarely does, keep the funnel cheap.

## 3. Change the persistent-market-failure treatment of `why_now`

This is the substantive scoring issue exposed by v1.5.

Under the current method, an archetype-B persistent market failure legitimately records `why_now.strength: absent`. At Plausible/Promising, v1.2 then caps both `problem_severity_frequency` and `differentiation` at 2.

That was sensible when a missing why-now represented an unsupported discovery story. It is not logically sound when the method **explicitly permits a different archetype whose thesis is that the problem has persisted for years**.

A recent discontinuity and evidence that a persistent market failure remains inadequately served are different evidence questions. Absence of the former should not mechanically cap the latter.

### Requested change

Do not remove the v1.2 cap globally.

Instead make the cap archetype-aware:

### Change-driven candidate
Retain the existing rule. If a claimed `why_now` is absent/weak and there is no Demand/Commercial evidence, the existing caps continue to apply.

### Persistent-market-failure candidate
Do not require or fabricate `why_now`. Replace that gate with an evidence-backed **persistence explanation**, e.g.:

> Why has this problem remained inadequately solved despite existing alternatives?

For the candidate to avoid the v1.2 caps, require affirmative evidence for the persistence thesis, not merely a narrative. Evidence should support both:

1. **continued buyer pain / cost / workaround despite available alternatives**, and
2. **a credible reason the existing market has not resolved it for this target segment**.

Examples of potentially adequate evidence:
- repeated recent practitioner complaints tied to the same narrow workflow;
- demonstrated manual workarounds despite incumbent availability;
- pricing/minimum-contract economics that systematically exclude the target segment;
- incumbent architecture/channel constraints that make the narrow job unattractive to solve;
- credible switching/data-access barriers combined with evidence buyers still incur the pain.

A statement such as "the market is too small for incumbents" without evidence is insufficient.

If that persistence evidence is absent or weak, retain the existing score caps.

This is not a bonus for evergreen ideas. It simply prevents "no recent event occurred" from being treated as evidence against a proposition whose defining hypothesis is persistent market failure.

### Regression requirement

Before adopting this change, re-evaluate at least:
- Aucly pre-launch;
- ShiftSwap;
- WonkyBox;
- GrantScout;
- one v1.5 persistent observation rejected at triage.

The rule must not resurrect generic crowded categories merely because complaints exist. `defensible_wedge` remains unchanged and can still kill an idea regardless of score.

## 4. One run is enough to retain the funnel provisionally, not to optimise it

The first run establishes that the funnel:
- can generate materially distinct observations;
- can reject obvious dead ends cheaply;
- does not necessarily manufacture three candidates;
- fits within a modest research budget.

It does **not** establish that 15–20 is optimal or that the source-class mix is optimal.

Retain the funnel for the next several runs and evaluate cumulative evidence. Consider resizing only if:
- lookup/step limits routinely prevent adequate shallow checks;
- pools show substantial duplication/padding;
- fewer observations repeatedly produce the same promotion quality;
- sampled false-negative audits reveal shallow triage errors; or
- additional observations near the end of the sweep repeatedly surface high-quality promotions.

## 5. Interpretation of the repeated `defensible_wedge` cluster

Do not weaken the filter.

The v1.5 run searched the previously requested messy classes and still found shipping competitors/free substitutes. That is useful negative evidence.

However, the conclusion "find seams where no product ships" is too strong if interpreted literally. A viable bootstrapped niche does not necessarily require zero competitors. Aucly itself is a useful calibration reminder: an opportunity can exist because alternatives are disproportionately expensive, awkward or poorly matched to a narrow segment.

The existing `defensible_wedge` semantics should therefore continue to ask whether there is a **credible reason this proposition can win/retain a narrow buyer despite alternatives**, not whether the category is literally empty.

Do not change the filter on this review; instead ensure discovery and triage do not equate "named competitors exist" with automatic failure unless the evidence shows they adequately occupy the proposed seam.
