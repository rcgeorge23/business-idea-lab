# Method review request: v1.2.0 (why-now cap scoped to pre-demand evidence)

Method version: 1.1.0 -> 1.2.0

Requested from: ChatGPT (independent reviewer, routed by the human owner)
Expected response file: `reviews/<date>-method-v1.2.0-chatgpt.md`

## What changed and why

Issue #3 added Aucly, a real launched business (charity/school auction platform, flat
fee, approximately 7 paid auctions reported by the human owner, real case studies), as a
calibration case. Under method 1.1.0 the why-now rule ("a missing why-now caps
`differentiation` and `problem_severity_frequency` at 2") applied unconditionally. Aucly
is an evergreen niche: school and charity fundraising auctions long predate the product,
so its why-now is `absent`, and the cap reduced `problem_severity_frequency` to 2 even
though there is direct payment evidence for the problem.

The change scopes the cap to ideas whose evidence level is `Plausible` or `Promising`
(pre-demand). From `Demand evidence` upward the cap does not apply, because observed
payment behaviour is stronger evidence than a discontinuity claim. Everything else is
unchanged. Full rationale: `method/CHANGELOG.md` (1.2.0), `ideas/aucly/decision.md`.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Is scoping the cap by evidence level the right fix, or does it create a loophole
   where a candidate claims demand evidence to escape scrutiny? Note the evidence-level
   definitions in `method/lifecycle.md` and that falsified or weak payment claims are
   still bounded by confidence and the `evidence_strength` dimension.
2. Does the change preserve the falsification character of the method? Specifically,
   killed fixtures must still be rejected for the same reasons.
3. Is the Aucly current-state score (60.0, parked below the unchanged 65 threshold)
   sensible, or does any dimension remain mis-scored in either direction? The
   scorecard and decision record are the artefacts to attack.
4. Is leaving all other scoring rules unchanged defensible, or is one of the inspected
   areas (small markets, bootstrapped vs venture, founder fit, cheap build/testability,
   direct competitors) a systematic miscalibration?
