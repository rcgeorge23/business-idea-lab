## What changed and why

Issue #4 followed run `20260921T075410Z-normal`, the first normal run under method
1.2.0. All three candidates (`vetcma` 61.0, `prsregister` 59.0, `propident` 49.0) had
strong, dated discontinuities and were killed on `defensible_wedge` because the obvious
first-order product responding to each change was already served by credible incumbents
or free public alternatives. The kills look correct; the concern is that discovery is
defaulting to "new rule/change -> compliance SaaS", which incumbents absorb quickly.

Three discovery-guidance changes are proposed (no scoring change):

1. **Second-order effects.** Before promoting a candidate, the worker must investigate
   the operational seam the discontinuity creates - manual handoffs, duplicate entry,
   reconciliation, evidence collection, exception handling, status chasing, awkward
   imports/exports, mandated data transformations, integration gaps between incumbents,
   workflow steps incumbents handle poorly, newly automatable human review, or
   underserved subsegments - and prefer an awkward operational seam to a generic
   compliance/dashboard product. It is explicitly not a requirement to invent a
   second-order opportunity; the worker may keep a first-order product if it records why
   the second-order options were weaker.
2. **Seed-aware discovery.** Normal runs must review the seed register at orientation
   and shallowly re-check the most relevant unexplored seeds against current evidence
   and incumbents. A seed may become a candidate only after the same research a fresh
   discovery would get, and never inherits the parent's score, evidence level,
   hard-filter results or assumptions. Runs remain explicitly not seed-only.
3. **Provenance and convergence recording.** Each candidate records provenance
   (`seed:<slug>` or `fresh`) and its second-order seam; the run summary must assess
   whether discovery is converging on less obvious, better-defended opportunities or
   repeating one class of rejection. A recurring `defensible_wedge` cluster is flagged
   for method review, not used to weaken the filter.

Full rationale: `method/CHANGELOG.md` (1.3.0), `method/discovery.md`.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Do the second-order categories actually capture where durable wedge lives for small
   operators, or are they a rebranding of the same first-order products (e.g. is
   "reconciliation" just a dashboard with a different label)?
2. Does the seed-aware path create a favouritism or anchoring risk - a seed's parent
   observation steering the "fresh" research - and is the no-inheritance rule (no score,
   evidence level, hard-filter result or assumption) sufficient to prevent it?
3. Does the mandatory convergence assessment risk pressuring future runs toward
   forced novelty (inventing a second-order seam where none exists)? Note the escape
   clause and the requirement to flag a `defensible_wedge` cluster for review instead.
4. Does this preserve the falsification-first character of the method? In particular,
   can a worker now game discovery to avoid `defensible_wedge` failures without any
   change in evidence quality?
