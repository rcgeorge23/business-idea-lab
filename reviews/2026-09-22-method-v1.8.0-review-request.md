The dominant rejection reason in the last five was **demonstrated occupation**: a
shipping product already served the exact seam. The convergence reviews
(`retrospectives/2026-09-21-issue6-convergence-review.md`,
`retrospectives/2026-09-21-issue7-funnel-review.md`) escalated this to a
sourcing/framing problem and explicitly said not to weaken `defensible_wedge`.

The diagnosis: complaint forums (HN, Stack Exchange, Discourse, GitHub issues) are
where problems are *discussed*, not where budgets are *spent*. A seam that is widely
discussed is, almost by definition, already visible to incumbents. The sweep was
systematically sampling the most-monetised part of the space.

## What changed and why

**1. A money-already-moving sourcing bias (`method/discovery.md`).** Every normal
funnel run must now aim a meaningful share of its search effort at sources where a
buyer is already paying, hiring or procuring:

- job advertisements describing repetitive administrative work;
- service and agency pricing pages;
- public procurement and tender records;
- incumbent support forums and release notes;
- trade press reporting spend or staffing;
- job descriptions for roles that exist only to bridge two systems.

The rationale is that these carry **budget evidence** that complaint forums do not: a
job ad for a re-keying role is evidence someone is paying a salary to do the work; a
consultancy charging for a manual process is evidence of an existing budget line; a
tender award is evidence of spend and of the buyer.

**2. A recording requirement.** The run summary must state which money-already-moving
sources were searched and what they yielded, including unsuccessful searches, and
whether the run found budget evidence or only complaint-forum evidence. A run that
cannot find budget evidence for a seam must record that as a finding rather than
treating complaint-forum observations as equivalent.

**3. The classes were added to the non-regulatory source-class list**, so a candidate
whose why-now comes from a money-already-moving source qualifies under the existing
budget without any new quota.

## Regression evidence

This is a **sourcing bias, not a scoring change**:

- weights stay 1.2.0; threshold stays 65;
- no cap is lifted, no evidence level is redefined, no hard filter is upgraded;
- `defensible_wedge` is unchanged and still fails where credible incumbents
  demonstrably occupy the exact proposed seam;
- killed fixtures (shiftswap 44.2, wonkybox 36.8, grantscout 55.8) and parked fixtures
  (geonerd 49.5, reasonable-steps 54.0, aucly 60.0, vetlab-en18029-conformance 44.2)
  are unaffected by construction because no score input changed;
- the empirical check is the sourcing-frame run performed under this version, recorded
  in `observations/` and the run summary.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Is the money-already-moving bias the right response to the convergence signal, or
   does it risk over-correcting toward "there is a budget, therefore there is an
   opportunity" when the budget may already be well spent?
2. Does requiring a meaningful share of effort at these sources create a new
   systematic blind spot - for example, missing genuinely new categories where no
   budget exists yet (the latent-opportunity route's territory)?
3. Is the recording requirement strong enough to prevent a run from claiming
   complaint-forum observations as budget evidence?
4. Are the regression arguments sufficient, given that no score input changed?
