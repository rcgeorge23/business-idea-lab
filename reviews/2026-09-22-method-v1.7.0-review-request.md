## Where this comes from

Issue #16 ("Add a guarded discovery path for latent, unarticulated opportunities")
identified a discovery-method blind spot: Method 1.6 is strong at articulated pain
(complaints, manual workarounds, spend, persistent market failures) but some worthwhile
products change a behaviour the buyer currently accepts as normal. The buyer may not
discuss a "problem" because they have not seen the alternative, so a sweep that only
looks for articulated pain can under-source or prematurely reject these hypotheses.

The issue is explicit that this is **not** a request to relax validation standards:
"people do not know they need it yet" must never become a blanket defence for an
unevidenced idea. Method 1.7.0 therefore adds a **guarded** route, not a new evidence
level or a lower bar.

## What changed and why

### 1. A third discovery archetype: latent opportunity (archetype C)

`method/discovery.md` (the archetype section is now "Three discovery archetypes"). An
observation may be recorded as a latent-opportunity hypothesis only if it names **all
six** of the following, each backed by dated observable evidence rather than model
imagination:

1. the specific buyer/user and their observed current behaviour or constraint;
2. the newly possible experience/capability and a concrete mechanism changing time,
   cost, quality, access or outcomes;
3. why the buyer might value the difference despite not requesting it (labelled as
   inference, with reasoning shown);
4. why now, or an honest statement that no discontinuity is known;
5. the existing substitute / status quo and direct or adjacent competitors;
6. the central falsifiable assumption and the cheapest behavioural test.

Source material for latent hypotheses is hypothesis-generating, not proof of demand:
observed adoption of adjacent tools, costly routines treated as normal, new
distribution/technical capabilities, workarounds in neighbouring markets, and revealed
behaviour when an alternative becomes available. Unsupported trend narratives and
AI-generated enthusiasm are explicitly not evidence.

### 2. Evidence and gate semantics (the guardrails)

`method/discovery.md` (archetype C), `method/evidence-policy.md` (new paragraph under
"Plausibility is not evidence"), `method/scorecard.md` (new note), `method/lifecycle.md`
(why-now section):

- Enough evidence to justify a **cheap test** need not include a pre-existing complaint
  or a search query for the proposed product.
- **Demand and commercial validation still require real target-buyer behaviour**:
  payment, a pre-order, a meaningful commitment, or an approved experiment with a
  pre-declared decision rule. Interviews asking "would you use this?" and model opinions
  do not suffice.
- A latent hypothesis with **no buyer, no observable status quo, no plausible
  distribution route or no decisive affordable test** is still rejected or parked.
- Missing evidence stays `unknown` and is **never** upgraded to a hard-filter `pass`.
- The route **lifts no score cap, lowers no threshold (65 unchanged), redefines no
  evidence level and upgrades no hard filter**. If the scorecard cannot represent such a
  hypothesis honestly, that is a separate reviewable method change with regression
  evidence - not something a run may do.
- Expensive external validation, outreach, publication and spend still require human
  approval under `method/experiment-rules.md`.
- For the missing-why-now cap, a latent candidate is treated like a
  persistent-market-failure candidate: no discontinuity required or invented, and the
  cap lifts only on an evidence-backed persistence thesis meeting both limbs.

### 3. Recording and templates

`method/run-protocol.md` (step 3.1 covers all three archetypes; step 3.3; step 12),
`AGENTS.md`, `scripts/prompts/lab-run.md`, `.opencode/agent/idea-worker.md`, and the
templates (`templates/observation/pool.md` gains a six-field latent block;
`templates/run/summary.md` gains the latent split and latent-route counts;
`templates/idea/dossier.md` and `templates/idea/decision.md` gain the latent archetype
and the six admissibility fields). A run that exercises the route reports how many
latent observations were found, triaged, promoted and rejected; zero promotions is
acceptable.

- **Killed fixtures remain rejected for the same reasons.** `shiftswap` (44.2),
  `wonkybox` (36.8) and `grantscout` (55.8) were re-read against the new wording: the

- **Named calibration examples** added to `method/calibration.md`: L1 (a plausible

- **Bounded sweep exercise.** The route was exercised in one bounded normal observation

- **Ledger audit.** All documented ideas, seeds and earlier triage rejections were

  silently changed.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Is the six-field admissibility test tight enough to stop "customers do not know they
   need it yet" from becoming a blanket defence, while still admitting a genuine latent
   hypothesis that has no prior complaint?
2. Is treating a latent candidate like a persistent-market-failure candidate for the
   missing-why-now cap the right choice, or should the latent route have its own
   explicit cap behaviour?
3. Does the route risk inviting argument at the margin in triage (an observation that
   would otherwise be rejected for no buyer now being dressed up as "latent")? If so,
   what additional guard would you require?
4. Are the regression outcomes, the named calibration examples and the ledger audit
   sufficient evidence that this version does not revive weak ideas by assertion or
   bypass `defensible_wedge`?
