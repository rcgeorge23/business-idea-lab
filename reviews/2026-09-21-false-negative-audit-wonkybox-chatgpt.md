# Independent false-negative audit response: WonkyBox (kill #15 trigger)

- **Verdict:** kill upheld

## Scope of review

This audit reviews the killed proposition **as scoped**: a local weekly DTC surplus/odd-produce subscription box at roughly £12, delivered or collected. It does not assert that every possible business involving surplus produce is unattractive.

The evidence reviewed was the dossier, decision record, scorecard and the two evidence files named in the request.

## 1. Was the original kill correct?

Yes.

The kill has multiple independent grounds rather than depending on a single pessimistic assumption:

- **Differentiation / wedge:** the direct consumer surplus-produce-box position is already occupied by Oddbox and another UK operator at low consumer price points. “Local” by itself is not an evidenced wedge.
- **Distribution:** the evidence records Oddbox stopping marketing to acquire new customers followed by falling revenue, while also reporting substantial churn. That does not prove every entrant must use paid acquisition, but it materially weakens the proposition's required low-CAC acquisition assumption.
- **Economics:** reported Oddbox accounts show persistent losses at approximately £27–32m revenue despite scale and substantial funding. This is strong negative category evidence for a smaller entrant attempting substantially the same delivered-subscription model.
- **Joint optimism:** a small entrant would need favourable acquisition, retention and route/delivery economics simultaneously while competing at a low ticket price.

The score of 36.8 and the hard-filter rejection are therefore directionally consistent with the evidence.

One wording in the original decision is stronger than the evidence warrants: Oddbox's losses are **not proof** that “a small entrant will not” succeed. The evidence file itself correctly says it does not prove no entrant can succeed and does not isolate the source of the losses. This overstatement does not change the kill because the decision rests on several adverse factors and the proposition has no affirmative evidence for a structural advantage.

## 2. Does pickup-only/community-hub distribution overturn the kill?

No, because that is a materially different mechanism whose economics were not established by the evidence reviewed.

A pickup/community-hub model could remove or reduce last-mile delivery cost and improve route density. It could therefore invalidate part of the adverse economics argument against **delivered** boxes.

But it also changes the customer proposition and introduces new unknowns: collection convenience, hub acquisition/operation, geographic density, retention, produce handling and whether enough margin remains at the observed £10–18 consumer price range.

The existing evidence explicitly says it does not establish whether such a differentiated model could work. Absence of evidence against that adjacent model is not evidence for it.

Therefore the correct interpretation is:

> Kill the DTC local surplus-box proposition as scoped; do not generalise the kill to structurally different pickup, community-hub or B2B surplus-distribution models.

## 3. Reopening condition

Reopen the killed proposition only if new evidence establishes a structural advantage relevant to its actual model, rather than merely renewed consumer interest in food waste.

For an adjacent pickup/community/B2B model, evidence worth considering would include independently verifiable small-scale economics showing positive contribution after produce, handling, spoilage, packaging, collection/hub or delivery costs, together with credible repeat purchase/retention and a plausible low-paid-acquisition route.

That would justify evaluating a **new proposition from scratch**, not changing WonkyBox's historical kill retrospectively.

## 4. Should an adjacent seed be created?

Not on the evidence currently in the repository.

“Pickup-only”, “community hub” and “B2B surplus” are possible mechanism changes, but the reviewed evidence contains no affirmative indication that one of them has attractive economics or an underserved buyer. Creating a promoted opportunity merely because it escapes one failure mode would risk turning the seed register into a list of speculative pivots.

If later discovery independently surfaces evidence for one of these models — for example a recurring buyer pain, demonstrated small-scale contribution economics, or a poorly served B2B surplus workflow — it should be captured as a fresh observation/candidate with its own provenance and evidence.

## False-negative conclusion

**Kill upheld.**

The audit does identify one calibration lesson: category-leader losses should be treated as strong negative evidence, not a universal impossibility proof. A structurally different business model can remain an open hypothesis without requiring the killed proposition to be reopened.

No scoring, hard-filter, lifecycle or method change is requested from this audit.
