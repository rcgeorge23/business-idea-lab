## Where this comes from

Issue #20 identifies a gap between the existing guarded latent-opportunity route and
how normal discovery runs: archetype C has six admissibility fields, but the funnel did
not require a deliberate search for behavior that may reveal an unarticulated benefit,
or a record when such a search produced no usable result.

The immediate calibration example is O16 in
`observations/20260925T115959Z-normal.md`. A CLCH NHS finance job advertisement posted
2026-09-08 describes invoice checking, provider/local-authority statement
reconciliation, work across Oracle/CareTrack/other systems, Excel/shared-inbox handling
and aged mismatches. It evidences staff behavior. It does not establish software-budget
authority, an inadequate incumbent, NHS-specific capability, a viable distribution
route or demand. The cited adjacent Rentari invoice capability is in US housing and is
not NHS validation. O16 was rejected and not promoted. Its source run predates this
rule and is not counted as a formally logged latent-signal pass.

## What changed and why

1. Every normal opportunity-observation sweep must conduct and log at least one bounded
   latent-signal search pass. It may reuse searches already conducted for the pool and
   must record the behavior-signal lens (what), source class (where), focus/query, date
   and yield, including no-result, blocked or unavailable searches and the reason.
2. Signal lenses describe behavior to look for: repeated shadow/manual work; work
   delayed, skipped or not attempted because of cost/complexity; handoffs,
   reconciliation, exceptions or shifted costs; recurring manual services/roles; and
   newly feasible tasks. Source classes describe where to look: public workflow
   artifacts/instructions, job descriptions, service scopes/pricing, practitioner or
   support discussions, release notes, procurement/tenders and trade/professional
   reporting.
3. This is a bounded search instruction, not a quota. Searches may be reused; there is
   no requirement to cover every lens or lens/source pairing, no minimum result or
   latent-observation count, and no extra candidate slot. A no-result search or absence
   of complaints is not evidence of non-consumption or demand.
4. Archetype C retains the same six fields. Factual observations require dated evidence;
   the buyer-value claim remains labelled inference; unverified mechanisms and buyer or
   payment claims remain inference/assumption. Neither inferred benefit nor novelty can
   establish demand, pass a hard filter, or justify promotion by itself.

## Regression evidence and non-changes

- This is a discovery search/recording change, not a scoring, evidence-level, filter,
  threshold, source-budget or lifecycle change. Weights remain 1.2.0 and the threshold
  remains 65.
- The observation pool remains 15–20; the candidate maximum remains three and the
  validation-ready maximum remains one. The source-class budget, lookup/step/cost/time
  limits and no-filler rule are unchanged.
- L1 remains admissible as a hypothesis that may justify a cheap test, but not demand
  evidence or a score lift; `vetlab-en18029-conformance` remains parked at 44.2. V1
  (busy-parent planner) and V2 (generic small-business process automation) remain
  rejected because their required fields cannot be supported. The unsupported claim
  “customers do not know they need it yet” remains insufficient.
- O16 remains rejected/unpromoted: observed work is a signal only; buyer authority,
  gap, fit, privacy/security and distribution remain unknown. No prior outcome is
  reinterpreted as a completed pass under this new requirement.
- No normal run has yet exercised version 1.8.2. A prospective compliant run should
  report pass completion/yield, including a no-result outcome, without manufacturing an
  observation or candidate.

## Counterarguments and failure conditions

- A mandatory pass may become ritualized box-checking or encourage filler. The no-quota
  rule and explicit no-result record are intended to prevent this; review whether the
  scope is sufficiently bounded under the existing run budget.
- Ordinary work may be mistaken for an unmet product need. The change intentionally
  treats behavior only as a hypothesis-generating signal and preserves all six fields,
  novelty checks, hard filters and buyer-behavior demand standard. Review whether that
  separation is explicit and operationally sufficient.
- Reusing sweep searches may make the new pass redundant or ambiguous. The log must name
  its lens and yield while avoiding a second search solely to satisfy the procedure.
  Review whether this yields useful coverage without duplicating effort.
- A lack of complaints/search results could be misread as proof that latent behavior is
  absent. The method expressly rejects that inference; review whether the wording and
  templates make the limit clear.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. Is one bounded pass per normal sweep a proportionate way to ensure deliberate
   coverage without creating an observation/candidate quota?
2. Are the behavior lenses and source classes distinct, concrete and sufficiently
   bounded for a worker to record reproducibly?
3. Do the no-result rules and the O16 calibration example adequately prevent job/work
   evidence or inferred benefit from being treated as buyer demand?
4. Do the six fields remain intact, with dated factual observations clearly separated
   from inference and assumption?
5. Are the no-scoring/no-filter/no-lifecycle regression claims and unchanged run limits
   accurate?
