## What changed and why

Motivated by GitHub issue #2 and the first normal run
(`runs/20260920T210550Z-normal`), which exposed two weaknesses:

1. generated candidates were conventional and had no recent discontinuity behind
   them (ShiftSwap, WonkyBox, GrantScout all lack a credible "why now?"); and
2. scores and hard-filter passes were too generous where the underlying material was
   an assumption (GeoNerd's `painful_frequent_or_budgeted` was marked `pass` with a
   note admitting it was "assumed ... by analogy"; economics scored 4 on a model with
   untested inputs).

v1.1.0 changes:

- **`method/discovery.md` (new):** the discontinuity test ("This was not an attractive
  business three years ago, but it might be now because ______ changed"), ten classes of
  change to investigate (legislation, consultations, new APIs/datasets, platform rules,
  incumbent disruption, new technical capability, manual data flows, poor narrow
  incumbents, mandated formats), the novelty/incumbent sanity check, duplicate detection
  and adjacent opportunity seeds.
- **`method/scorecard.md` (weights 1.1.0):** scores measure demonstrated strength;
  plausible-but-undemonstrated caps at 2; explicit caps for analogy-based problem,
  inferred budget, plausible-only distribution, modelled economics (<=2, low
  confidence); direct incumbent overlap caps differentiation at 2; a feature of an
  established category fails `defensible_wedge`. Hard filters now carry
  `status` (pass | unknown | fail), `note`, `evidence` and `resolve_via`; `pass` requires
  affirmative cited evidence; assumptions and analogies cannot pass; `unknown` is
  permitted at any lifecycle state, contributes no evidence, and must be named in the
  proposed experiment at `validation-ready` (new threshold condition 8).
- **`method/evidence-policy.md`:** "Plausibility is not evidence".
- **`method/lifecycle.md`:** transitions aligned to the new semantics; experiments are
  explicitly decoupled from `validation-ready` ("Experiments while parked") so a parked
  idea can still reach a cheap decisive test.
- **`method/run-protocol.md`:** steps reordered around discontinuity hunting and the
  novelty check; source classes recorded in the run summary.
- **`method/review-policy.md`:** method-change handling; re-request after a revision
  change.
- **Templates:** dossier gains "Why now?" and "Novelty / incumbent sanity check";
  scorecard hard filters gain `evidence`/`resolve_via`; new `templates/seed/entry.md`;
  run summary gains source classes and why-now quality.
- **Ledger:** GeoNerd rescored 65.3 -> 49.5 and moved down from `validation-ready` to
  `adversarially-researched` (see `reviews/2026-09-21-geonerd-review-request-v2.md`);
  `why_now` backfilled for the three killed ideas; GrantScout adjacent seed recorded.
- **Validator:** new filter semantics enforced, `why_now` required, `seeds/index.json`
  checked, historical run method versions downgraded from error to warning.

## Questions for the reviewer

1. **Threshold rigidity.** The 65 threshold was deliberately NOT lowered even though
   the tightened rubric makes desk-only ideas land in the 45-60 range. Is that the right
   call, or should `validation-ready` be gated primarily by no-fail + named experiment
   rather than an aggregate score? The worker's position: do not move goalposts; use
   "Experiments while parked" to let promising untested ideas reach a cheap test.
2. **Unknown semantics.** Is `unknown` (with `resolve_via`, allowed at any state,
   contributing no score) a sound way to distinguish "plausible" from "demonstrated",
   or does it create a loophole that lets weak ideas survive to review?
3. **Non-inheriting seeds.** Does `seeds/` (no score, confidence or evidence level;
   cannot be promoted without fresh research) adequately preserve observations from
   killed ideas without resurrecting them?
4. **Any regression.** Does anything in 1.1.0 weaken the rejection machinery (hard
   filters, adversarial pass, preserved kills, pre-fixed decision rules, advance
   limits, false-negative audits)?
